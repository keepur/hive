# KPR-455 — The operational reader, and the inbound acknowledgement edge

## TL;DR

KPR-455 is the surface a human uses to see what the ops record already knows and to act on one thing it is showing them. It ships one CLI (`hive ops health`, `hive ops stalled`, `hive ops ack`), one informational `hive doctor` section, and nothing else: no collection of its own, no periodic sweep, no `index.ts` wiring, no dashboard, no policy row. Two of its three parts are pure reads over stores its siblings own — KPR-454's `ops_events` for the tool-health rollup, `activity_log` for the quiet-turn view, KPR-468's heartbeat for pipeline freshness — they create nothing and write nothing, and every one of them answers `unknown` past a staleness horizon rather than `healthy`. The third part is KPR-458 D6's **inbound acknowledgement edge**: `hive ops ack` translates one operator act into one `accept(...)` call on KPR-468's intake, attributes it or refuses it, and reads no ledger — though it does call the sibling's own `init()`, which materialises `ops_notifications` and its indexes on a cold instance (D8, D12). That edge is what makes KPR-468 shippable — the notifier must not go live without it. **What this child does not deliver, stated up front: no push path for stalled turns.** Nothing publishes turn-outcome conditions into `ops_events`, so no subscription can match them and the notifier can never nudge on them; after this merges, that half of the ticket is visible when a human asks and by no other means (D3, D5).

## Key Points

- **The reader reads; the edge captures and calls.** Neither holds policy. There is no owner table, no severity, no recipient, no cadence, no escalation and no new `hive.yaml` key in this child, and it registers zero rows of KPR-458 D11's third column — the same posture KPR-456's canon fixes for an engineering child, followed here without exception. The one policy-shaped value that does live in code is the per-view default staleness horizon, named as such in D6 so "ships zero policy" is not read more literally than the diff supports.
- **The viewer is a CLI plus one small doctor section, and deliberately not a channel.** `hive obligations` is the precedent for the CLI: a read-only operator surface over a durable store, `--json`, cursor paging, sentinel-verified, no `init()` on read paths. It shipped *without* a doctor section, and this child deliberately differs on exactly one point — the ops pipeline's own liveness goes on the surface an operator already runs, because this epic exists precisely because a feed that has gone quiet is indistinguishable from a healthy one, and that failure mode applies to the ops feed itself (D2). The "one muted channel" candidate is **not built**: under KPR-458 a channel is a subscription row plus KPR-468's Slack transport — operator data over shipped code, not a thing this child writes.
- **No periodic sweep ships, and the reason is that a timer here would have no legal output — not that a push path covers it.** Every view is computed on demand from indexed reads. D10's invariants (a) and (b) leave a sweep in this child unable to deliver, publish or spawn a turn, so its only legal output is a rendering nobody is watching; KPR-468 owns the epic's only ops sweep. The push path an operator can register is real for **published** conditions (tool health today) and **does not exist for the quiet-turn arm at all** — see the TL;DR. For that arm the surface is pull only, and a cron over the CLI produces the same unwatched output this section rejects as grounds for the timer (D3).
- **The tool-health rollup reads `ops_events` and can read nothing else.** `activity_log` carries `toolCalls` and a `name×count` `toolSummary` (`src/activity/types.ts:25-26`) and **no per-tool outcome at all**, so it cannot produce this view; KPR-454's `detail.tool` / `detail.errorSig` is the only source, exactly as KPR-458 D8 assumes. Latest outcome is resolved on the `(publishedAt, _id)` total order, and a `tool-recovered` fact clears **every** `errorSig` row for its tool, because KPR-454 D5 gives the recovery reason no `errorSig` key — an asymmetry that must be stated, not discovered (D4).
- **Stale means `unknown`, and absence never means healthy.** A tool with no fresh fact is `unknown`; a tool that appears nowhere in the log is not reported at all, and the output says in its own words that not-reported is not healthy. The horizon is a flag with a code-side default, and the default direction is safe by construction: a shorter horizon yields more `unknown` and can never manufacture a `healthy` (D6).
- **The stalled-work half has two arms, and only one of them has data today.** The contract's work-status view (KPR-458 D8, keyed `subject`, open until a class-legal clearing fact) is implemented **generically and is empty**: no chartered producer publishes `workItem`-subject conditions — KPR-454 keys deliberately on the tool, and widening `system:task_blocked` is out of its scope. The arm with data is the measured one from this ticket's own scope addition: `error ∨ aborted ∨ timedOut` over `activity_log`, 8.9% of 1,480 turns on 2026-09-08 and 9.1% on a slid window, already in the record and surfaced to nobody (D5).
- **The ticket's `owner`-null and `costUsd` premises are both superseded, and neither quietly.** KPR-458 D2 lists `owner`, `escalatedTo`, `assignee` and `severity` as fields no consumer may add, so "`owner` null" is unrepresentable and ownership is expressed as a subscription; and `costUsd` is a literal `0` for every Lane B turn at `src/agents/provider-adapters/turn-scaffold.ts:353` (verified at this tree), so no view here reads cost or duration as an outcome signal (C12).
- **"Page Mokie" is a subscription row for published conditions, and for quiet turns it is nothing at all.** Under the contract, paging a named person is `ops_subscriptions` + a transport binding — D11 column 3, operator work, and the canon forbids this child supplying a default recipient. That answer is genuine for the tool-health half; for the quiet-turn arm no subscription can match, because nothing publishes those conditions, so the honest answer is that this child makes them *visible on demand* and the missing piece is a producer (D5, Assumptions).
- **`matchedSubscriptions` is rendered as matched-at-publish, with its instant, and never as a present-tense claim.** The field is stamped at accept and is honest only for the subscription set that existed then (KPR-458 C2/D2/D5). "Claimed by nobody" would be a present-tense reading of a past-tense field, and the reverse — a nonzero stamp whose subscription has since been deleted rendering as *claimed* — is the stale-positive defect this epic exists to remove (D5, AC8).
- **The edge is `hive ops ack`, and it is one edge on purpose.** It passes its own invocation instant as `at` at full precision — for a CLI the invocation *is* the vendor act, so there is no receipt time to substitute and no precision to truncate — requires `--actor` with no default and no `$USER` fallback, offers no `--at` flag, carries the opaque handle KPR-468's Slack transport already prints as `ref:` in the message body, reads `snoozedUntil` only when `state === "snoozed"`, and treats `refused: "row-cleared"` as benign (D7). **`unavailable` means UNKNOWN, not "did not apply"** — KPR-468's intake says so in a paragraph written for this edge — so the edge retries the **identical** `(actorId, act, at)` tuple with `at` minted once, which is what makes `lastAckKey` collapse a landed act to a no-op instead of extending a snooze twice. A Slack button edge is the natural second one and is **not** built here: KPR-468's adapter posts plain text with no `blocks`, so buttons would be a cross-child edit to a `ready-to-implement` sibling's renderer (D7, Assumptions).
- **The edge reaches intake by hosting it, and that is the call a reviewer should press hardest.** The CLI is a separate process from the engine, so it constructs KPR-468's notifier against the same database and calls `accept(...)` — the seam, unchanged, with no ledger access of the edge's own. It also **overrides a sibling's explicit scoping statement** — KPR-468 D8's "cross-process concurrency is out of model" — which is quoted, argued and flagged for the epic driver in Scope and authority rather than silently absorbed. Two named divergences follow and both are argued in D8: the in-process per-row latch does not span processes — KPR-468's CAS is what actually detects the race and returns `unavailable` rather than blending, **and the cost is two-sided**, because the engine's own post-attempt delivery-record write can now lose its CAS and is deliberately never repaired (an outcome the single-process model made impossible); and the snooze clamp falls back to the contract-side hard ceiling because no tick has retained a policy copy — vacuous while `ops_policy` ships zero rows, and never unbounded.
- **In scope:** the `hive ops` CLI (three subcommands), the tool-health rollup, the two stalled-work arms, the staleness rule and its flag, the acknowledgement edge, one informational doctor section, `CLAUDE.md`. **Out of scope:** any dashboard or web UI (Gate 1, and the operator's deferral ruling), any collection of this child's own, any **ledger view** (nothing here reads `ops_notifications`, so a handle is discoverable only from the delivered message — Non-goals), any periodic sweep, any subscription/cadence/horizon/reason row, any transport, any Slack button or agent-facing MCP tool, any change to `ops_events`/`ops_notifications`/`ops_subscriptions`/`ops_reasons`/`activity_log`/`agent_events`, and any change to KPR-454's or KPR-468's code.

## Problem

Three gaps, each measured rather than asserted.

**The failures are in the record and reach no human.** `activity_log.error` is non-null on 74 of 1,480 turn rows (trailing 7d on `hive_dodi`, queried 2026-09-08); `timedOut: true` on 65, of which **57 carry `error: null`**; `aborted: true` on 57. The truthful union `error ∨ aborted ∨ timedOut` is 131 — **8.9%, against the 5.0% `error` alone reports** — re-confirmed at 127/1,392 (9.1%) on a slid window. Cite the ratio and the date, never the bare count. Those flags are written sparsely at `src/agents/agent-manager.ts:2611-2612` (KPR-401) and are read by nothing an operator ever sees.

**The heuristic the brief proposed is unusable.** `src/agents/provider-adapters/turn-scaffold.ts:353` returns a literal `costUsd: 0` in the shared Lane B `RunResult` builder — verified at this tree — so every Lane B turn reports zero cost, and the longest zero-cost run in the measured window was a 27-minute *successful* turn. Cost is a billing field; on a subscription lane it carries no outcome information. C12 makes this a rule: **outcome is published or read from a published flag, never inferred from cost or duration.**

**A quiet feed and a healthy feed look identical.** That is the epic's thesis, and it applies to the ops machinery itself. KPR-454's publisher exposes counters only through an in-memory `getSnapshot()` with no telemetry document, so from outside the engine "no tool failures" and "the publisher was never set" are the same observation. KPR-468's sweep can wedge on one poison event with `ingestFaults` climbing at a static `cursorAt`. A reader that renders an empty rollup as good news reproduces, inside the fix, the exact defect the fix exists to remove.

## Goals and non-goals

**Goals.** Let a human answer three questions from a terminal, with no agent turn and no engine change: *which tools are failing, and which are merely unknown*; *what started and went quiet*; and *is the ops pipeline itself alive and current*. Let that same human acknowledge one notification they were shown, attributed, through the notifier's own intake. Never assert health from absence. Never store, print, or forward a raw error string, a message body, or a credential-shaped value.

**Non-goals.** A dashboard or web UI of any kind — the ticket forbids it and the operator deferred it explicitly ("a dashboard is inevitable but deferred; Gate 1's bar stands, and the contract is shaped so a dashboard is later a client over the same store rather than a rewrite"). A periodic sweep. A second inbox. Any collection, index, or write to `ops_events`, `ops_subscriptions`, `ops_reasons`, `activity_log` or `agent_events`. Any subscription row, transport binding, cadence value, nudge floor, snooze maximum, staleness-horizon *policy row* or reason row (D11 column 3). Any recipient, severity, escalation ladder, owner model or de-escalation path. A Slack interactive edge, an agent-facing acknowledgement MCP tool, or any second edge. **No ledger view of any kind** — and this one is stated because KPR-468 D12 charters *every* reader to this child, so an unstated omission here is exactly how this epic's "chartered to nobody" holes formed: this child reads `ops_events`, `activity_log` and `db.telemetry` and **never reads `ops_notifications`**, so nothing lists pending, delivered, seen, dismissed or snoozed rows, nothing shows a row's attempt history, and **a handle is discoverable only from the delivered Slack message's `ref:` line**. The consequences are real and are carried elsewhere in this spec rather than hidden: an operator who loses the message cannot look the handle up, and D7's `snoozed`-on-`unavailable` residual says "re-check" with no view to re-check in. A ledger reader is a coherent follow-up child and is not this one. Any change to KPR-454's or KPR-468's shipped code. Any `hive.yaml` key. Any `index.ts` wiring or `boot-order.test.ts` anchor.

## Design

### D1. Shape: three surfaces, one process, no state

This child ships one CLI module and one doctor section. It owns no collection, no singleton, no timer and no in-process state, and it is not wired into the engine at all.

| Surface | What it is | Reads | Writes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hive ops health` | the tool-health rollup (b) plus pipeline freshness | `ops_events`, `db.telemetry` `ops_notifier_stats` | nothing |
| `hive ops stalled` | the stalled-work view (a), two arms | `ops_events`, `activity_log`, `db.telemetry` `ops_notifier_stats` | nothing |
| `hive ops ack <handle>` | the KPR-458 D6 inbound acknowledgement edge (c) | nothing of its own — though KPR-468's `init()` calls `loadReasons()`, which reads `ops_reasons` inside the sibling's code (D8) | one `accept(...)` call; intake writes one ledger row. It also calls KPR-468's `init()`, whose `ensureIndexes()` materialises `ops_notifications` and its indexes on a cold instance — the sibling's schema, by the sibling's code (D8) |
| `hive doctor` §Ops event pipeline | bounded liveness, informational | `db.telemetry` `ops_notifier_stats`, `ops_events` recency | nothing |

**Neither read path reads `ops_reasons`, and that is a contract fact rather than an omission.** `class` and `retry` are **stored on every event**, copied from the registry entry at accept and never producer-supplied (`kpr-458-design.md:100-101`), and KPR-454 stamps both into its insert (`:360`). D5's class-legality rule turns on the *cleared condition's* `class` (`kpr-458-design.md:323`), which is therefore a field of a document the reader already has. So the two read paths' read-sets are exactly what their rows say. The one `ops_reasons` read anywhere in this diff is KPR-468's own `loadReasons()`, inside `init()`, on the `ack` path — the sibling's code, priced in D8 — which is why the `ack` row names it and the other two do not.

**The rule that governs all four, stated once:** the reader reads and the edge captures and calls. Neither decides who should hear about anything, how loud it is, when it should be repeated, or whether it matters. Every such decision already has a home — the subscription row, the cadence table, the reason registry — and none of them is this child's.

**Nothing here is a per-spawn read.** The CLI is a separate process and the doctor section is a separate process; no turn reads either. This child therefore adds **no wiring above `src/index.ts:474`'s `// ── Spawn-capable boundary ──` marker and no anchors to `src/boot-order.test.ts`**, and the boot-order rule is satisfied vacuously rather than by care. That is worth stating because both siblings do add anchors, and a reader sweeping the epic for consistency will look for this child's and correctly find none.

### D2. The viewer: a CLI, plus one doctor section, and not a channel

The ticket names three candidates and asks for the cheapest. All three are priced against what the contract actually leaves for this child to build.

**A CLI, and `hive obligations` is the precedent to copy.** `src/cli/obligations.ts` is a read-only operator surface over a durable store, and every one of its structural choices transfers: `selectInstance` for explicit instance/config selection (`:23-63`), `verifySentinel` before any read so a query cannot silently answer from another instance's database (`:131-135`), `WriteGuard`/`guardDb` on the write path, `parseArgs` with `strict: true`, opaque base64url cursors with a section+owner check (`src/obligations/reader.ts:19-39`), a `--json` flag that selects compact over indented JSON, and the comment at `src/cli/obligations.ts:139` — "No init() on read paths" — which this child reproduces exactly (D8). The subcommand shape mirrors it too: `list`-like breadth (`hive ops health`, `hive ops stalled`) beside a targeted act (`hive ops ack`), dispatched from `src/cli.ts` on the pattern at `:173-181`, with its usage lines added beside the `obligations` block at `:140-143`.

**The output shape is JSON on every path, and every sentence this spec promises is a string field.** The precedent emits one JSON document and nothing else (`src/cli/obligations.ts:185` — `deps.emit(JSON.stringify(result, null, parsed.values.json ? undefined : 2))`, so `--json` selects compact over indented), and it carries prose in a named string field rather than in a rendering: `policy: "Deadlines at or before the cutoff remain due…"` (`:173`). This child does the same. So **"absence is not health", "the window was truncated by retention", "no turns matched in this window", "the ops event log does not exist on this instance" and the `matchedAtPublish` labelling are string fields on the result object** — assertable by a test on the payload, not dependent on a rendering nobody has fixed. Stdout carries exactly one JSON document (D8's `setLogLevel` note is what keeps that true on the `ack` path). This is what makes AC3, AC7 and AC11 string-assertable rather than aspirational.

**One `hive doctor` section, and this is the deliberate divergence from that precedent.** KPR-456 shipped its CLI with no doctor section, and copying that would be the cheaper answer by one file. It is declined for a reason specific to this epic rather than to taste: **an ops pipeline that has stopped is invisible until somebody thinks to look at it**, and "thinking to look" is exactly what fails in the incident this epic was filed about. KPR-468 names its own worst case — a deterministically-faulting event wedges ingest, `eventsBehind` and `oldestUnappliedAt` grow without bound, and if it stands long enough the queued events age past the event TTL and are lost — and states that the heartbeat is the only signal. Putting that signal on the surface an operator already runs, in five or six lines, is the epic's own thesis applied to the epic's own machinery. The section is **informational and never flips the exit code**, per KPR-296 canon that only the Datastore identity section may; it renders liveness, freshness and backlog and carries **no per-condition detail**, which stays in the CLI.

**Not a muted channel.** Under KPR-458 a channel is not a thing this child can build: delivery to a Slack conversation is a `ops_subscriptions` row plus KPR-468's `SlackOpsTransport` binding, which is operator data over already-shipped sibling code. Building a channel writer here would mean either a second transport (barred — KPR-468 owns the one adapter) or a catch-all destination (barred outright by C3 and by the no-default-recipient canon). The honest statement is that the muted channel **already exists as a deployment option the moment KPR-468 merges**, needs no code from this child, and is how "page Mokie" is expressed (D5).

**Not a web UI.** Gate 1 forbids it and the operator deferred it with the shape argument attached: the contract is built so a dashboard is later a client over the same store rather than a rewrite. Nothing in this spec is easier or harder because of that; it is simply not built.

### D3. No sweep — where "Mokie's scheduled sweep" went

The ticket's title says *scheduled sweep*, and this child ships no timer. That is a supersession with a reason, not an omission.

The ticket predates KPR-458 and KPR-468. When it was written, "the sweep" was the only mechanism in the proposal: something had to run periodically, notice stalled work, and page a person. Under the contract those are now three separate things with three separate owners — **noticing** is the producer's publish (KPR-454), **repeating until acknowledged** is the notifier's bounded 30-second sweep and its cadence table (KPR-468), and **paging a named person** is a subscription row (operator, D11 column 3). What is left for this child is *showing*, and showing is a query.

A periodic timer here would therefore compute a rendering and have nowhere to put it: it may not deliver (KPR-468 owns delivery), may not publish (D10 invariant (a) — the ops path never publishes about itself), and may not spawn a turn (invariant (b), C14). A sweep whose only legal output is a log line is cost with no consumer, and the epic's own ruling — "Dashboard = V2… Not another inbox to patrol" — is the same instinct.

**What an operator does instead — and for part (a) that is pull only, stated plainly rather than covered by a push path that does not reach it.** The push path is real but its reach is exactly the set of conditions a chartered producer publishes into `ops_events`: today that is KPR-454's **tool** conditions and nothing else. A subscription matches published events, so for the stalled-work half it can match arm 1 (empty — no producer publishes `workItem`-subject conditions) and **cannot match arm 2 at all**: quiet turns live in `activity_log`, nothing publishes turn-outcome conditions into `ops_events`, and therefore no subscription can select them and the notifier can never nudge on them. After this child merges, the honest statement about the ticket's part (a) is that **stalled work is visible when a human asks, and by no other means** — a cron over the CLI is the same unwatched output this section rejects as grounds for a timer, not a substitute for a push path.

The no-sweep decision does not rest on that push path and never did: it rests on D10's invariants (a) and (b) leaving a timer here **no legal output**, and on KPR-468 owning the epic's only ops sweep. A push path for (a) requires a *producer* publishing turn-outcome conditions into `ops_events` — a new chartered child, outside KPR-454's tool-keyed scope and outside this diff. ⚠ Named as a gap in Assumptions rather than implied away.

For the tool half (b), and for arm 1 the day a producer fills it, the push path is genuine: register a subscription and the notifier nudges on a cadence. For everything else the surface is the CLI, which invokes no agent and holds no state — so a cron over it is a shell command, which is what KPR-456's canon means by exposing evidence "without invoking agents".

### D4. (b) The tool-health rollup

**Key and source.** `(detail.tool, detail.errorSig)` over `ops_events` documents whose `producer` is `hive-runtime` and whose `reasonId` is a tool condition — KPR-458 D8's view, unchanged. The source is fixed, not chosen: `activity_log` has no per-tool outcome field at all — `toolCalls` is a count and `toolSummary` is a `name×count` string (`src/activity/types.ts:25-26`) — so it cannot answer this question, and `ops_events` is the only store that can. This is also why the rollup is **empty until KPR-454 has been running**, which the output states rather than implies.

**Latest outcome, resolved on the log's own total order.** For each key the reader takes the most recent document by `(publishedAt, _id)` descending — the same total order KPR-454's epoch resolver and KPR-468's cursor use, and the order KPR-454's `{ producer, subject.kind, subject.id, reasonId, publishedAt: -1, _id: -1 }` index already covers. Outcome mapping:

| latest fact for the key | rendered outcome |
| --- | --- |
| a tool-condition event (`tool-failed`) inside the horizon | `failing`, with `errorSig`, `lane`, event count, and first/last instants |
| a clearing event (`tool-recovered`) for that tool inside the horizon | `recovered`, with the instant |
| the latest fact is older than the horizon | **`unknown`** |
| the key does not appear in the log at all | not listed — and the output says absence is not health |

**The recovery-key asymmetry, stated because it is not discoverable from the key.** The rollup is keyed `(tool, errorSig)` but KPR-454 D5 gives `tool-recovered` a `detailKeys` of `tool` and `lane` only — a recovery carries **no `errorSig`**. So a recovery cannot be matched to one row of the key space, and the rule is that **a recovery for a tool resolves every `errorSig` row for that tool**. That is not a compromise: KPR-454 D4 already accepts that all error tokens for one tool share one condition ("A tool that times out for agent A and rejects agent B's input is one row"), and the tool coming back is a fact about the tool. An implementation that quietly dropped recoveries because they failed to join on `errorSig` would render every recovered tool as permanently `failing`, which is the most likely wrong implementation and is what AC4 exists to fail.

**Absence is not health, and the reader says so in its own output.** Three distinct emptinesses must not read alike, and each gets its own line rather than a shared blank: *no `ops_events` documents at all* (the publisher has never run here, or the log has TTL'd); *documents exist but none inside the horizon* (every key is `unknown`); *documents exist and all keys resolved to `recovered`* (the only case that is genuinely good news, and it is still a statement about the tools that appear in the log, not about the fleet). The rollup also prints the age of the newest `ops_events` document of any reason as **evidence of last-known publisher activity, explicitly labelled as not a health signal** — a healthy fleet legitimately publishes nothing for days, so recency is a lower bound on when the publisher was last alive and never an upper bound on health. The honest gap this leaves is named in D9.

**Bounds, stated honestly rather than by the flag that does not provide them.** `--limit` caps **output rows**, not documents scanned, and saying "bounded by `--limit`" would be false: KPR-454 appends one event per failure, so a key-space enumeration over the horizon scales with **event volume inside the horizon**, ceilinged only by the `ops_events` TTL. Two things are therefore fixed here. The rollup's **per-key latest read is covered**: one indexed read per key on KPR-454's `{ producer, subject.kind, subject.id, reasonId, publishedAt: -1, _id: -1 }` index (`kpr-454-design.md:386`), in the same `(publishedAt, _id)` order the resolver compares, never an in-memory sort over the window. **One clause of precision on that:** the rollup keys on `detail.tool`/`detail.errorSig`, and `detail.tool` carries the same canonical tool name as the indexed `subject.id` (`kpr-454-design.md:10`), so the tool half is reachable as an indexed equality — but `detail.errorSig` is **not** a key of that index, so the read is index-*ordered* with `errorSig` as a residual predicate. For a tool carrying many distinct `errorSig` values the walk to one key's latest document is bounded by the horizon but is not O(1). The **key-space enumeration is the honest cost**: a bounded aggregation over the horizon whose read scales with events published inside it, not with rows printed — so the horizon and the event TTL are what bound this command, and a caller narrowing `--limit` does not narrow the work. Stated so a plan writer sizing this does not read `--limit` as a scan bound.

### D5. (a) The stalled-work view — two arms, one of them empty

The ticket's (a) reads: "`blocked` with no same-run follow-up past TTL and `owner` null → page Mokie". Three of its four terms were re-expressed by the contract and one of them no longer exists. Taking them in order rather than paraphrasing them away:

- **`blocked`** is, under KPR-458 D8, the derived *work-status* view: keyed `subject` where `subject.kind = "workItem"`, open until a class-legal clearing fact, `unknown` past a horizon. The reader implements it — **generically over any `subject.kind`, not only `workItem`**, since the contract's rule is per view and the mechanism is identical.
- **"no same-run follow-up"** is the clearing fact. The contract replaced same-run correlation with published provenance for a stated reason: KPR-453 canon is that a work-item identity "proves neither unique attempt nor task continuity", so "same run" is not a property a reader can establish from the record. What it *can* establish is that a class-legal clearing fact was published against that condition's `dedupeKey`, which is exactly D8's rule and is stronger.
- **"past TTL"** is the staleness horizon, D6.
- **"`owner` null" is unrepresentable and must not be reintroduced.** KPR-458 D2 lists `owner`, `escalatedTo`, `assignee`, `severity`, `destination`, `channel` and `recipient` as fields explicitly absent and "not to be added by any consumer". Ownership under the contract is expressed by *who subscribed*: a condition with a matching subscription has a ledger row and someone being nudged; a condition with `matchedSubscriptions: 0` has nobody, and that count is the countable gap C3 preserves. **The reader renders `matchedSubscriptions` as a past-tense fact and never as a present-tense claim** — see the labelling rule below, which is what keeps this from becoming the stale-positive defect the epic exists to remove.

**`matchedSubscriptions` is matched-at-publish, and is labelled as such.** The field is stamped **at accept** and is honest only for the subscription set that existed at that instant (KPR-458 C2, D5, and D2's row: "the stored count stays honest for the set that existed at accept"). So the reader renders it as **`matchedAtPublish: <n> (as of <publishedAt>)`**, never as "claimed by nobody" or any other present-tense phrasing. **The rendered name is deliberately distinct from the stored one**, so it is not read as a schema claim: the value is the stored scalar `matchedSubscriptions`, which is the length of the stamped `matchedSubscriptionIds` array written in the same immutable insert (`kpr-454-design.md:358`, `kpr-458-design.md:110-111`); the reader renames it at the rendering boundary and adds nothing to the event. Both directions matter and the second is the worse one: a zero rendered as a live "nobody is on this" is a present-tense claim from a past-tense field, and a **nonzero** stamp whose subscription has since been disabled or deleted renders as *claimed* when nobody is listening — a false reassurance, which is exactly the reading this spec's "absence is not health" posture refuses everywhere else. The reader cannot resolve either direction, because resolving it means reading `ops_subscriptions` against the current set, which is a live-matching judgment this child does not hold. What it owes is the honest label and the instant, and the output says in its own words that the count describes the moment of publication.

**Arm 1 — open conditions, specified and empty.** For each `dedupeKey` with a condition event inside the horizon and no class-legal clearing fact after it: the subject, reason, `class`/`waiting`/`retry`, `generation`, event count, first and last instants, `matchedAtPublish` with its instant, and `unknown` past the horizon. It is correct today and returns **only tool conditions**, because KPR-454 keys deliberately on the tool and no chartered child publishes `workItem`-subject conditions — widening `system:task_blocked` is out of KPR-454's scope, and `system:task_blocked` itself is publishable only through the agent-facing event-bus MCP server (`src/events/event-types.ts:119-125`; no engine writer exists at this tree) into a collection KPR-458 D10 declines for this purpose. This is stated rather than hidden: the arm is the shape the epic's Storage section asked for, it is generic, and it will populate the day a producer publishes into it. ⚠ Named in Assumptions.

**"No class-legal clearing fact after it" names a rule, and here is its mechanism — decided, not hedged.** KPR-468's predicate cannot be borrowed in the shape this reader needs: `clearingProvenanceOk(row: OpsNotification, e: OpsEvent)` (`kpr-468-plan-2b-ingest.md:123`) reads `row.producer` and `row.class` off a **ledger row**, and this child never has one — it may not read `ops_notifications` at all. **The decision is that the reader applies the rule itself, over two events, and does not request an event-shaped export from KPR-468.** Three reasons. (1) It is this child's charter, not a borrowed authority: KPR-458 D8 *defines* the derived view as "open until a class-legal clearing fact", so applying class legality is the view's own definition — one rule with two appliers **by contract**, which is a different thing from D8's refusal to let the *edge* pre-judge acts intake owns. (2) The export would have to be reshaped to take an event pair, which is a cross-child edit to a `ready-to-implement` sibling — declined here exactly as the other two cross-child remedies in this spec are. (3) The drift risk finding 7 names is real in both directions and is closed by pinning the source and the test rather than by copying code: **KPR-458 D3 is the single source**, and the reader's predicate is its four class outcomes verbatim — same `producer` on both events, the later event's `clears` naming the condition's `dedupeKey`, then `resource` ⇒ legal, `judgment`/`integrity` ⇒ legal only with `evidence.length >= 1`, `informational` ⇒ never legal. That is `clearingProvenanceOk`'s body, and AC16 enumerates all four so a drift fails a test rather than silently changing a rendering.

Two consequences worth stating. The `class` the predicate reads is the **cleared condition's** (`kpr-458-design.md:323`), and it is a field of a document the reader already has: `class` and `retry` are copied from the registry entry into every event at accept and are never producer-supplied (`kpr-458-design.md:100-101`), and KPR-454 stamps both into its insert (`:360`). **So neither read path reads `ops_reasons` at all** — D1's table is complete as it stands. The residual is forward-compatibility rather than a missing row: an event whose `class` is a value this reader does not recognize is **fail-open-as-open** — the clearing fact is not treated as class-legal, the condition stays listed, and its class renders `unknown`. That is the sibling's own arm, not an inference: `clearingProvenanceOk` ends `case "informational": default: return false` (`kpr-468-plan-2b-ingest.md:131-133`), so the reader and the ledger fail the same way on the same input, which tightens the drift-pinning above rather than relying on it. Note the vocabulary is contract-fixed, not registry data — `kpr-458-design.md:370` gives `class`/`waiting`/`retry` a `—` in both the producer and the operator column — so a fifth class is a contract revision rather than a row somebody registers; an absent `class` on a non-conforming document funnels to the same handling. That is the safe direction for this epic — a reader that showed *cleared* on an unresolvable class would be the "absence is not health" defect wearing a different hat. And ⚠ if KPR-468 later exports an event-shaped predicate, this reader should adopt it and delete its own; recorded in Assumptions.

**Arm 2 — quiet turns, the arm with data.** Over `activity_log`, restricted by `TURN_ACTIVITY_FILTER` (`src/activity/types.ts:58`) so KPR-456's delivery receipts are excluded, within a window: turns matching **`error ∨ aborted ∨ timedOut`**, grouped by `(agentId, channelKind)` with counts per outcome flag, plus the most recent N rows rendered as `agentId`, `threadId`, `timestamp`, `model`, and *which flags were set*. This is the union the scope addition measured and is precisely what C12 requires — a reader that flagged all 289 Lane B rows on a cost heuristic, or that missed the 57 `timedOut` rows carrying `error: null`, fails that criterion, and AC6 drives both limbs.

**Arm 2 renders no error text.** The `error` field is an arbitrary string written by whatever threw, so the reader prints **that the flag was set**, never its content. Three reasons, and the first is the operative one: C12 says `error` is not a failure oracle, and printing it as the headline of a failure row is exactly the reading C12 forbids; KPR-458 D9's rule that a diagnostics surface is where a token gets written applies with full force to a surface whose purpose is pasting output into a chat window; and the responder's next step is the thread and the agent, both of which are printed. A caller who genuinely needs the string reads the row in Mongo, deliberately.

**Two arms, one command, and they are not merged.** They answer different questions from different stores with different keys and different horizons; a combined "stalled work" number over both would be an invented aggregate with no meaning, and inventing one is the shape of a severity axis. They render as two labelled sections of one output.

**"Page Mokie", concretely — and it is only half an answer, so both halves are stated.** This child pages nobody. Under the canon it may not: no default recipient, no escalation, no production registration.

For a condition that **exists as an `ops_events` document**, the operational answer is a real one: an `ops_subscriptions` row naming a filter and a Slack conversation, plus a cadence entry in `ops_policy` — both operator data, both landing the moment KPR-468 merges, and both outside every line of this diff. That covers the tool-health half (b) and arm 1 the day arm 1 has data.

For **arm 2 it is not an answer at all**, and saying it is would be false. Quiet turns are `activity_log` rows; nothing publishes them into `ops_events`; a subscription selects published events; therefore no subscription can match them and no notifier can nudge on them. There is no push path for the ticket's part (a) after this child merges — it is **pull only**. What this child owes there is that the condition is *visible on demand and honestly labelled*, and that is the whole of what it delivers; the missing piece is a producer, named as a gap in D3 and in Assumptions rather than dressed as an operator configuration step.

### D6. The staleness horizon — a flag, a safe default, and no new key

C11 requires a derived view to return `unknown` past a horizon, and KPR-458 D8 makes two things contract — a view must *have* one, and the horizon is a property of the view rather than of a reason, a class or a subscription — while making the horizon's **value** operator data (D11 column 3), of which this child ships zero rows.

The resolution is a `--stale-after <minutes>` flag per read command, defaulting to a code-side constant per view (⚠ delegated values: one for the tool-health rollup, one for the work-status arm, one window for the quiet-turn arm). **The default's direction is what makes this safe rather than a dodge**: past the horizon the answer is `unknown`, so a *shorter* horizon yields more `unknown` and fewer positive claims. No default value can manufacture a `healthy`, which is the single failure mode C11 exists to prevent — the safety property holds for every setting, and the flag only trades precision.

**One clause of honesty about that constant, so "ships zero policy" is not read literally.** A default horizon *is* a policy value, and it lives in code here. What the canon forbids is a *registered* policy row — a D11 column-3 subscription, cadence, nudge floor, snooze maximum or reason — and this child registers none; the constant is a per-view rendering default with no persistence, no operator visibility beyond `--help`, and no effect on any other component. That is a materially smaller thing than a policy row, and it is not nothing. Stated because a reader auditing "zero policy" against the diff will find this constant and should find it already named.

Two alternatives are declined and the reasons are worth recording. Reading the horizon from KPR-468's `ops_policy` document would put a policy read in a reader and would be a cross-child edit to a `ready-to-implement` sibling for a value that document does not currently hold. A `hive.yaml` key is declined under the standing "no preemptive levers" preference and matches both siblings, which ship none.

### D7. (c) The inbound acknowledgement edge

**What it is.** `hive ops ack <handle> --act <seen|dismissed|snoozed> --actor <id> [--until <iso8601>]` converts one operator act into exactly one *act* on KPR-468's intake — one `accept(...)` call, or that identical call repeated unchanged on `unavailable` (below) — and does nothing else. It reads no ledger, reads no `ops_subscriptions`, holds no state, and holds no policy — every judgment about the act (idempotency, transition legality, `integrity` dismissal, the snooze clamp) belongs to intake, which is what makes a duplicate invocation safe and the edge memoryless.

**Conformance to KPR-468 D7's "what the edge owes", clause by clause:**

| Obligation (KPR-468 D7) | How this edge satisfies it |
| --- | --- |
| convert one vendor act into one `accept(...)` call and do nothing else | **one *act* per invocation**, never a second act: no batching, and no call whose `(actorId, act, at)` tuple differs from the first. The one permitted repeat is the identical tuple on `unavailable`, which KPR-468's intake asks for by name (see below) and which `lastAckKey` makes a no-op if the first landed |
| never read or write the ledger or `ops_subscriptions` | the edge issues no query of its own; intake performs the read and the CAS (D8) |
| hold no state | the process exits after one act; nothing is persisted between invocations, and the retry's only carried value is the `at` it started with |
| attribute or refuse | `--actor` is **required**, has no default and no `$USER` or hostname fallback; a missing or blank value is refused locally, before any connection |
| pass `at` as the vendor act's own instant, at full precision, never a receipt time, never truncated | for a CLI the **invocation is the act**: `at` is a `Date` read once at command parse, at full millisecond precision, and there is no upstream instant to preserve or lose |
| read `snoozedUntil` only when `state === "snoozed"` | the result renderer reads the echoed `snoozedUntil` only on an `applied` result whose `rowState` is `snoozed` |

**No `--at` flag, deliberately.** Offering one would let a caller assert an instant, and although intake anchors it (`anchorAt = min(at, now)`) so nothing unbounded follows, an edge that accepts a caller-supplied act time is manufacturing an act it did not witness — which is the same bar D6 sets against read receipts, channel visits, reactions and elapsed time. The only instant this edge can honestly report is the one it was invoked at.

**Attribution is a record, not an authorization.** KPR-468 D7 is explicit that the handle "names a row and confers no authority — D9's model is attribution, not authorization". `--actor` is therefore a stable identifier the human supplies for themselves (a Slack user id, an agent slug); the edge checks only that it is present and non-blank, and passes it through. Every judgment about the *value* — including the refusal of the reserved `system:ops` principal, which KPR-468 D7 step 2 performs — belongs to intake, and duplicating it here would be the second enforcer of one rule set that KPR-468's own D12 argument warns against.

**Where the handle comes from.** The opaque handle rides `NotificationView.handle` and KPR-468's Slack transport already prints it as a `ref: <handle>` line in the delivered message body (its renderer's last line). An operator reading a notification in a channel therefore has the handle in front of them, and the edge parses nothing out of it — the value is copied verbatim and is opaque by contract.

**Rendering every result, and the one that is benign.** The command emits a JSON object and sets an exit code:

| `OpsIntakeResult` | rendered | exit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `applied` | `rowState`, and the **clamped** `snoozedUntil` echoed back, so the human is told what actually happened rather than what they asked for | 0 |
| `noop: "already-applied"` / `"superseded"` | stated plainly as a no-op, not an error | 0 |
| `refused: "row-cleared"` | **benign** — KPR-468 D7 step 4 fixes this: the condition resolved between the delivery and the click, nothing is recorded, and the edge must not surface it as an error the human has to handle | 0 |
| `refused: "unattributed"` / `"unknown-handle"` / `"integrity-dismissal"` / `"snooze-not-future"` / `"illegal-transition"` | the contract token verbatim | 1 |
| `unavailable` | **UNKNOWN — not "nothing was recorded"**; the edge has already retried the identical tuple (below) and reports that the act *may* have applied. Exit 1 means **not confirmed**, not *failed*, and the rendered text says so | 1 |

**The refusal token goes in the JSON payload, not in a thrown error message.** `src/cli.ts:178` prints a thrown `err.message` only when it matches `/^[a-z_]+$/`, and KPR-468's refusal vocabulary is kebab-case (`row-cleared`, `integrity-dismissal`), which that guard would swallow into a generic failure string. The thrown-error path stays reserved for CLI-local failures under the existing snake_case convention (`invalid_command`, `actor_required`, `until_required`, `identity_unverified`, and D8's `notifier_init_failed`); contract refusals are data in the output.

**On `unavailable`, the edge retries the *identical* tuple, because KPR-468 says so in a paragraph written for this edge.** `kpr-468-plan-4-intake.md:307-314` is explicit: *"`unavailable` means UNKNOWN, NOT 'did not apply', and it is SAFE TO RETRY. The deadline below abandons the work rather than cancelling it, so a write can land after the caller has already been told `unavailable` — and a retry of the SAME `(actorId, act, at)` is the correct response, because `lastAckKey` makes it return `{ state: "noop", reason: "already-applied" }` if it did land and apply it if it did not. An edge that treats `unavailable` as a failure and surfaces an error to the human is reading it wrong."* The contract-side document says the same thing in its own words — *"a duplicate callback is safe and the edge needs no memory"* (`kpr-468-design.md:425`) — so the plan is not widening D7's "one act and do nothing else": that clause constrains the **key**, and D7 itself already presumes retries exist (*"a substituted receipt time makes every **retry** a distinct key"*, same line). The rule follows exactly:

- **`at` is minted once, at command parse, and is held fixed across every call in the invocation.** That is what makes the repeat a *retry* rather than a second act, and it is the only mechanism — `lastAckKey` — that keeps a possibly-applied `snoozed` from extending the pause a second time. **A re-run by the human is not this**: a fresh invocation mints a fresh `at`, derives a different idempotence key, and is a genuinely new act.
- **The retry is bounded and short** (⚠ delegated: a small attempt count with brief backoff). It does not need to span KPR-468's 11 s delivery-latch window, because that window is not what this edge hits — see the mechanism note below.
- **On exhaustion the result is rendered as UNKNOWN, exit 1**, naming the act, the handle and the fixed `at`, and stating that it may have applied. `seen` and `dismissed` are effect-idempotent, so a human re-run of either is harmless whatever happened. **`snoozed` is the one act a fresh re-run can move at all** — a new `at` derives a new key, so it is a genuinely new act rather than a replay — but the residual is much smaller than "double-extend", and overstating it would be the argument for a scope addition this spec does not make. `--until` is an **absolute** instant, and intake's rule is that re-snoozing an already-`snoozed` row *moves* `snoozedUntil` and nothing else (`kpr-468-design.md:421`), clamped to `min(requested, anchorAt + min(HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING, policy.maxSnoozeMs ?? HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING))`. So an identical re-run with the same `--until T` yields **the same `T`** whenever `T` is inside the ceiling — the normal case, and no extension at all. The residual survives only where the operator asked for longer than the ⚠ 7-day hard ceiling, and there the second run extends the pause by the human's *own re-run delay*, still bounded at the ceiling measured from that second act. **And it is repairable with no ledger view:** `snoozed` is a legal from-state (`kpr-468-design.md:403`) and `seen`/`dismissed` **unset** `snoozedUntil` (`:417`), so one further `hive ops ack` on the same handle ends an over-long pause. The rendered guidance for `snoozed` is therefore to re-check before re-running — with no ledger view in this child (Non-goals), "re-check" means the notifier's next nudge or a deliberate Mongo read — and to use that one further act if the pause did land longer than wanted.

**Which `unavailable` this edge actually meets, stated precisely because the two mechanisms differ.** `kpr-468-plan-4-intake.md:316-326` names a *deterministic* one: `withDeadline` wraps `withRowLock`, the delivery phase holds that per-row latch for up to 11 s, so an acknowledgement landing on a row whose delivery is slow returns `unavailable` **every time**. That latch is **in-process**, and this edge hosts its own notifier in a second process with its own latch map (D8) — so the CLI does not queue behind the engine's latch and does not meet the deterministic path. What it meets is the **lost CAS**: one retry inside intake, then `unavailable` (`kpr-468-design.md:417`). Same token, different mechanism, and the same instruction — retry the identical tuple — which is why the rule above is written to the token and not to either mechanism. An in-engine edge (a future Slack callback) would meet the deterministic path instead and needs the same rule for the other reason.

**One edge ships.** A Slack button edge is the natural second one and is not built: KPR-468's `SlackOpsTransport` posts plain text with `mrkdwn: false` and no `blocks`, so buttons would require changing a `ready-to-implement` sibling's renderer, plus enabling Interactivity on the Slack app and adding an `interactive` handler to `SlackGateway` (whose Socket Mode client currently registers `message`, two `assistant_thread_*` handlers and a `slack_event` catch-all at `src/slack/slack-gateway.ts:273`). An agent-facing MCP tool is also not built: it would open an MCP surface subject to both KPR-390 standing obligations and to `WORKER_SERVER_DENYLIST`, and an agent relaying "the human said dismiss it" is manufacturing an act D6 requires to be the human's own. Both remain addable later without redesign — D6 contemplates several surfaces — and ⚠ the Slack variant is offered to the operator as a named non-blocking follow-up in Assumptions.

### D8. How the edge reaches intake

The CLI is a separate process from the engine; KPR-468's `accept(...)` is a method on a module-global singleton inside it. The edge therefore **constructs KPR-468's notifier against the same database and calls `accept(...)` on it** — the seam itself, unchanged, invoked from a second process. The edge still touches no ledger: the read and the CAS are intake's code, running under intake's rules.

**The path, mirroring `hive obligations` exactly.** `selectInstance` resolves config/instance and the Mongo URI/db (`src/cli/obligations.ts:23-63`); `verifySentinel` must return `verified` or the command fails `identity_unverified` (`:131-135`) — the same guard that keeps a query from answering out of another instance's database, and it matters more on a write path; `guardDb` under a `WriteGuard` wraps the handle (`:136-137`). `init()` runs **only on the `ack` path**, never on the two read paths, reproducing the precedent at `src/cli/obligations.ts` — the comment "No init() on read paths" at `:139`, and `store.init()` called on `register` alone at `:144`. `init()` is required rather than optional: KPR-468 D10 fixes `{ state: "unavailable" }` for a notifier whose `init()` did not complete. `registerTransport` is never called and `start()` is never called — intake depends on neither, and starting a sweep from a CLI would be a second deliverer.

**What that `init()` actually does, because it is not free and this spec previously said it was.** KPR-468's `init()` runs `this.store.ensureIndexes()` (`kpr-468-plan-3b-notifier.md:144-149`), which creates `ops_notifications` and its nine indexes — including the `{ expiresAt: 1 }` TTL — and **throws** when the unique identity index fails. Three consequences, all fixed here:

- **The collection and its indexes are materialised by the `ack` path on a cold instance.** They are the *sibling's* schema, created by the sibling's own idempotent code, and this child defines none of it — but "no collection or index is created on any path in this diff" would be a false operational statement, and D12 now says the accurate thing instead. The two **read** paths remain what the original claim describes: no `init()`, no creation, no write.
- **A throwing `init()` has its own reported failure and must never be reported as `unknown-handle` or as `unavailable`.** It is the one `init()` fault that means *the ledger's identity index is unavailable*, and collapsing it into a refusal token would tell the operator their handle was wrong when their database is. The edge reports it as a CLI-local failure `notifier_init_failed`, exit 1, before any `accept(...)` call — snake_case deliberately, so `src/cli.ts:178`'s `/^[a-z_]+$/` guard passes the token through rather than swallowing it.
- **`init()` re-runs KPR-454's registry audit, and its output is silenced on stdout rather than left to corrupt the JSON.** `loadReasons()` re-emits `auditReasonRow` warnings and recompiles a zod schema per row (`kpr-468-plan-3b-notifier.md`, the ⚠ note under `init()`), and this repo's logger writes everything below `error` to **stdout** (`src/logging/logger.ts:22`) — so an unguarded `ack` would interleave audit warnings with its own JSON document and break every `| jq`. `runOps` therefore calls `setLogLevel("error")` (`src/logging/logger.ts:7`) at entry on all three subcommands: warnings are suppressed, genuine errors still reach stderr, and **stdout carries exactly one JSON document on every path**. Nothing is hidden that an operator needs — the registry audit belongs to the engine's own boot, where it is already emitted. One clause of honesty: `setLogLevel` sets a module-global that is seeded from `LOG_LEVEL` (`src/logging/logger.ts:5`), so this **silently overrides an operator's `LOG_LEVEL` for the life of the process and is not restored** — acceptable for a short-lived CLI whose contract is one JSON document on stdout, and applied uniformly across all three subcommands rather than on `ack` alone so the property is not path-dependent. The precedent `runObligations` sets nothing, so this is a deliberate divergence rather than a copy.

**Two divergences follow from the second process, and both are named rather than assumed away.**

**(i) The per-row latch does not span processes, and the CAS is what actually carries the property.** KPR-468 D8 orders an intake write against a concurrent sweep tick with an in-process `Map<rowId, Promise>` latch *and* a CAS on every precondition the write depends on, and states the guarantee as "serialized, never merged… a lost race is *detected* rather than blended". Across processes the latch is absent and the CAS is not: intake's step-8 write is one conditional `updateOne` on `state`, `lastAckKey` and `lastAckAt` with `w: "majority"`, which retries once and returns `{ state: "unavailable" }` on a lost race. So the *safety* property — no blended write, no out-of-order application — is carried by the CAS in both topologies; what the latch adds is ordering under contention. **The cost of its absence is two-sided, and the operator-facing half is the smaller one.**

- **The caller's loss** is at most one `unavailable` on a row a tick touched in the same instant — resolved by the identical-tuple retry D7 now specifies, which usually clears it inside the same invocation.
- **The engine's loss is the reciprocal, and it is not the caller's to retry.** When intake wins that race, the delivery phase's post-attempt write loses *its* CAS (`matchedCount === 0`), and KPR-468 deliberately does not repair it: *"the attempt HAPPENED, the record did not land, and inventing a second write against a row another writer just moved is how a half-applied state gets created"* (`kpr-468-plan-3-delivery.md:478-480`). `attemptCount`, `lastOutcome` and `nextNudgeAt` for that attempt are lost and counted only, on the `deliveryRecordLost` counter (`:482`). **Under the in-process latch this outcome was unreachable by construction.**

The honest statement is therefore: **no blended write in either topology, at the cost of a droppable delivery-record write that the single-process model made impossible** — bounded to the attempts that collide, visible in a counter the sibling already keeps, and never a half-applied row. **This is the single claim in this spec a reviewer should press hardest**, and AC10 drives it against the real precondition rather than a stub.

**(ii) The snooze clamp falls back to the contract-side hard ceiling.** Intake's clamp is `min(requested, anchorAt + min(HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING, policy.maxSnoozeMs ?? HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING))`, and `policy` is "the copy the last tick retained, never a fresh read". No tick runs in a CLI process, so the `?? HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING` branch applies — which KPR-468 D7 already enumerates as one of that branch's two covered paths ("no tick having completed yet"). The consequence is exact: a clamp always exists and nothing is ever unbounded, but an operator's *registered* maximum, being tighter than the hard ceiling, is not applied on this path. It is **vacuous until an operator registers one**, since `ops_policy` ships zero rows. ⚠ Non-blocking, with the one-line sibling remedy named (KPR-468 priming its policy copy in `init()`), declined here because it is a cross-child edit to a sibling that is not yet implemented.

**What is deliberately not done.** The edge does not query `ops_notifications` to pre-validate the handle, pre-check the transition, or pre-clamp the snooze. Each would be the edge holding policy, D6 bars all three, and each would produce a second enforcer of a rule set intake already owns.

### D9. What the reader never renders, and the gap it cannot close

**Redaction carries to the terminal.** C13's prohibitions are written about stored fields, and this child stores nothing — but the surface whose purpose is producing text an operator pastes into a chat window is exactly where a token escapes, which is the epic's own §5.1 argument. So the reader renders, from every source: identifiers, enumerated tokens, counts, instants and durations. It never renders `activity_log.error`'s string, a `toolSummary`'s content beyond the tool names it already is, a message or prompt or completion body, a tool argument, a URL, a file path, or free-form operator prose. `evidence` references render as `kind:id` pairs — pointers a responder opens, never join keys (KPR-453 canon).

**The honest gap: publisher liveness is unobservable from outside the engine at this tree.** KPR-454 D9 exposes its counters through an in-memory `getSnapshot()` and deliberately ships no telemetry document, no doctor section and no heartbeat. So no out-of-process reader can distinguish "no tool failed" from "the publisher was never set" — the second being the state KPR-454 D10 reaches when its registry upsert faults. Three things follow and all three are stated in the output rather than discovered: the rollup's `unknown`-past-horizon rule already holds without this signal, and it is the rule that makes the view safe; `newestEventAt` is reported as last-known publisher activity and explicitly labelled *not* a health signal, because a healthy fleet legitimately publishes nothing; and the remedy — an `ops_publisher_stats` heartbeat on the KPR-456 pattern — is a cross-child addition to a sibling that is not yet implemented and belongs to the epic driver, not to this diff. ⚠ Named in Assumptions.

**The doctor section's own single-sample limitation.** KPR-468's wedge signature is `ingestFaults` climbing *while* `cursorAt` stands still — a rate against a static value, which one read cannot see. The section therefore warns on what a single sample does show: `state: "degraded"`, a heartbeat older than 120 s (the threshold every existing section uses), a non-zero `ingestFaults`, and an `oldestUnappliedAt` whose age is a material fraction of `config.activity.retentionDays` (`src/config.ts:659`) — an age against an age, which KPR-468 D5 names as the direct measure of the TTL-outrun gap. The limitation is printed alongside rather than papered over: two runs a minute apart distinguish a blip from a wedge.

**`state` has three values, and the middle one is rendered, not warned.** KPR-468's heartbeat carries `ok | backlog | degraded` (`kpr-468-design.md:282`: all phases succeeded and no backlog ⇒ `ok`; all succeeded *with* backlog ⇒ `backlog`; any phase failed ⇒ `degraded`). `backlog` means the sweep is working and behind — a normal, self-correcting state after a burst — so it renders **informationally**, with `eventsBehind` (read as "at least N", since KPR-468's count saturates at a limit) and `oldestUnappliedAt` printed beside it, and it does **not** by itself produce a warn. What escalates a backlog is the independent condition already in the list: an `oldestUnappliedAt` aged against `config.activity.retentionDays`, which is the TTL-outrun measure and warns whatever `state` says. A `backlog` that never clears is what two runs a minute apart are for. Neither value, and no combination of them, ever flips the exit code.

### D11. Levers and rollback

**No `hive.yaml` key ships**, matching both siblings and the standing "no preemptive levers" preference. Every knob is a flag on an invocation: `--stale-after`, `--window`, `--limit`, `--cursor`, `--json`, `--instance`/`--config`.

**Rollback is a code revert.** There is nothing to turn off: with no `ops_events` the reads return empty-and-labelled, with no notifier heartbeat the doctor section prints `○ no heartbeat yet`, and the edge is only reached when a human types it. The one operational consequence of reverting is the sequencing constraint below.

**The sequencing constraint, restated because it is load-bearing.** **KPR-468 must not go live without this child's edge.** Without a way to acknowledge, `judgment` and `integrity` rows and never-recovering `resource` conditions nudge without bound, with no stop but KPR-468's per-subscription kill switch. Recovering `resource` conditions fall silent on their own — KPR-458 D4's enable gate guarantees every `resource` reason has a registered clearing reason, and a clearing fact reaches `cleared` with no acknowledgement. The two need not merge together; the notifier must not be *deployed with subscriptions and a cadence* first.

### D12. Boundaries — what this child deliberately does not touch

- **KPR-454 and KPR-468 are read-only from here.** No file under `src/ops/` is modified. Both remedies this spec names for them (an `ops_publisher_stats` heartbeat; priming intake's policy copy) are declined as cross-child edits and recorded as assumptions.
- **This child defines no collection, index or TTL of its own, and issues no write of its own** — but the accurate operational statement is narrower than "none is created", and D8 fixes it: on the `ack` path `init()` runs KPR-468's `store.ensureIndexes()`, which materialises `ops_notifications` and its nine indexes (including the `{ expiresAt: 1 }` TTL) on a cold instance, using the sibling's own idempotent code and the sibling's own schema. The **two read paths** are what "creates nothing, writes nothing" describes literally: no `init()`, no creation, no write. The only ledger write in the entire diff is intake's own CAS, performed by KPR-468's code, on the `ack` path.
- **No default subscription, recipient, adapter or catch-all** is created on any path (C3). A condition whose `matchedSubscriptions` was zero at publish is rendered as `matchedAtPublish: 0` with its instant, which is the measurement, not a defect to route around.
- **No agent is invoked, no turn is spawned, nothing is published** (C14, D10 invariant (a) and (b)). The ops path never publishes about itself, and this child has no publish call at all.
- **`agent_events`, the scheduler, `activity_log`'s writer, KPR-456's obligation path and KPR-457's Beekeeper sender are unchanged.** `subscribe: string[]` on an agent definition stays the event-bus domain list and is never overloaded to mean an ops subscription.
- **No MCP server is added**, so both KPR-390 standing obligations are inapplicable and are named so a reader does not go looking: there is no `buildToolTransportInventory` descriptor to push and no `suppressAutoInjectedServers` gate to extend.

## Edge cases

1. **`ops_events` does not exist** (KPR-454 not deployed) — the reads return an empty result labelled "no ops event log on this instance", never an empty rollup that reads as clean.
2. **`ops_notifications` does not exist** (KPR-468 not deployed) — `init()` **creates** it and its indexes (D8), the handle lookup finds no row, and the answer is `refused: "unknown-handle"`, exit 1. It is *not* `unavailable`: intake is initialised, so its own guard does not fire. The separate case where `init()` **throws** — the unique identity index could not be created — is reported as `notifier_init_failed`, never as `unknown-handle` and never as `unavailable`, because "your handle is wrong" and "your ledger's identity index is unavailable" are different diagnoses. The reads are unaffected on all three branches.
3. **No notifier heartbeat** — the doctor section prints `○ no heartbeat yet — start the engine and re-check`, matching `renderPrefixCacheSection`'s wording (`src/cli/doctor.ts:108`), and no health claim is made.
4. **A stale heartbeat with a healthy-looking `state`** — `ObligationReader.heartbeat()` (`src/obligations/reader.ts:47-57`) already solves this: past 120 s the state is reported as `unknown` regardless of what the document says. The doctor section and the read commands do the same, and it is the precedent to copy verbatim rather than re-derive.
5. **A recovery with no preceding failure inside the horizon** — rendered as `recovered` with its instant; the reader does not synthesize a failure it cannot see.
6. **A condition whose reason row was disabled after publication** — the events stand and render normally; `enabled` gates publication, not reading.
7. **A `--stale-after` longer than the `ops_events` TTL** — the horizon cannot exceed what the log retains, so results are bounded by the TTL, and the output says the window was truncated by retention rather than silently returning less.
8. **`--act snoozed` with no `--until`** — refused locally as `until_required` before connecting, since a snooze with no target is not a pause; a `--until` that is present but not in the future is passed through and refused by intake as `snooze-not-future`, because *judging* the value is intake's job.
9. **A handle for a row in another instance's database** — `verifySentinel` fails the command before any call; the handle is never used to reach across instances.
10. **A concurrent sweep tick on the same row** — intake retries its CAS once, then returns `unavailable`; the edge retries the **identical** `(actorId, act, at)` tuple (D7) and normally resolves inside the same invocation. On exhaustion the result is rendered as UNKNOWN, not as "nothing was recorded". The reciprocal — the tick's own delivery-record write losing its CAS and not being repaired — is the engine-side cost priced in D8(i), and is not something this command can see or resolve.
11. **An `activity_log` window with zero matching turns** — reported as "no turns matched in this window" with the window stated, never as "no failures".
12. **Activity logging disabled** (`ActivityLogger` returns early at `src/activity/activity-logger.ts:29-32`) — arm 2's silence is meaningless, so the command reports the window as unmeasurable when the collection is absent or empty for the whole window rather than reporting zero failures.

## Assumptions and remaining decisions

- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — the CLI hosts intake in its own process.** The edge constructs KPR-468's notifier and calls `accept(...)` from a second process. Argued in D8: the CAS carries the safety property in both topologies and the in-process latch only adds ordering under contention. **This is the claim to review hardest.** The alternative — a new authenticated endpoint on an engine HTTP server so the CLI could RPC into the running singleton — is declined on cost and surface: `AdminApi` (`src/admin/admin-api.ts:49-56`) is optional, token-gated and scoped to agent CRUD, and the background-task and code-task servers are neither operator surfaces nor general ones. Reversal is contained to one module.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — the CLI-path snooze clamp uses the contract-side hard ceiling, not an operator-registered maximum.** D8(ii). A clamp always exists and is never unbounded; the divergence is vacuous while `ops_policy` ships zero rows, and the one-line remedy belongs to KPR-468.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — one edge ships; the Slack button edge is offered, not built.** It is the better long-run surface — a vendor-attested Slack user id and an `action_ts` with sub-second precision are exactly what KPR-468's `at` and attribution obligations are written for — but it needs Block Kit accessories in KPR-468's renderer (which today posts plain text with no `blocks`), Interactivity enabled on the Slack app, and an `interactive` handler on `SlackGateway`. **The operator may simply ask for it as a follow-up child; nothing in this design has to change to accept it.**
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — no periodic sweep.** D3. The ticket's title says "scheduled sweep"; the scheduling now lives in KPR-468's cadence or in an operator cron over this CLI. Reversal would be a new timer with no consumer.
- **⚠ Cross-child fact for the epic driver — KPR-468 D8's "cross-process concurrency is out of model" is overridden by this child's edge.** Recorded and quoted in Scope and authority, with the CAS argument for why it is safe and with the three mechanisms that sentence names (new work identity, lease, elapsed-time reclamation) confirmed still absent. Not a KPR-455-local decision: if the override is unwanted, the declined RPC-into-the-engine alternative is the fork, and D7/D8 change with it.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — the edge's identical-tuple retry budget on `unavailable`** (a small attempt count and brief backoff). The *rule* is not delegated and is fixed in D7: `at` is minted once and held fixed, so a retry is never a second act. The named residual is `snoozed` specifically, and it is smaller than "double-extend": `--until` is absolute and re-snoozing *moves* `snoozedUntil` (`kpr-468-design.md:421`), so a re-run with the same `--until` yields the same instant unless the request exceeded the ⚠ 7-day hard ceiling — where the second act extends the pause by the human's own re-run delay, still bounded from that act. - **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — the reader applies KPR-458 D3's class-legality rule itself rather than borrowing KPR-468's predicate.** Decided in D5: `clearingProvenanceOk` takes a ledger row this child may not read, reshaping it is a cross-child edit to a `ready-to-implement` sibling, and KPR-458 D8 makes class legality part of the derived view's own definition. Quiet turns live in `activity_log`; nothing publishes turn-outcome conditions into `ops_events`; a subscription selects published events; so no subscription can match them and the notifier can never nudge on them. After this child merges, stalled work of that kind is visible **only when a human runs the command**.
