# KPR-468 — The notifier: ledger, delivery, nudge, clearing and acknowledgement intake

## TL;DR

KPR-468 is the wire between KPR-454's publish and KPR-455's read. It owns `ops_notifications` — the acknowledgement ledger KPR-458 D7 specifies — and the bounded 30 s sweep that mints and renews rows from the stamped `matchedSubscriptionIds`, hands each to an `OpsTransport` adapter, nudges on an operator-registered cadence, expires snoozes, and applies clearing facts; plus **acknowledgement intake**, the one non-swept path, which is the seam KPR-455's inbound edge calls. It holds no policy: it re-evaluates no filter, authors no cadence, resolves no recipient, and ships zero rows of KPR-458 D11's third column. D12's sequencing constraint against KPR-455 **stands**; two properties soften it from a code hazard to a deployment one — with no subscription rows nothing is ever created, and with no registered cadence a row is **attempted once per occurrence (delivered if the transport accepts) and never nudged between occurrences** (its first appearance, each recurrence after a clear, each expiry of a snooze — and nothing else) — leaving the one residual D11 names in full: an operator who registers both a subscription *and* a cadence before KPR-455's inbound edge lands reaches exactly the combination KPR-458 D12 calls "not honest staging", stoppable only by the per-subscription kill switch.

## Key Points

- **Two collections, not three.** `ops_notifications` (D1's third contract collection, this child's to create) and `ops_policy` (a single operator-written document holding the D8 cadence table, D5's named profiles, the minimum-nudge floor and the snooze maximum — **zero rows shipped**). The D12 sweep cursor is deliberately **not** a collection: it is one `db.telemetry` document (`kind: "ops_sweep_cursor"`), beside the sweep's own heartbeat (`kind: "ops_notifier_stats"`), which is the surface KPR-455 reads for freshness and backlog.
- **Idempotence is a watermark, not an event-id equality test — and the contract's own phrasing is not sufficient.** D12 says the upsert "is idempotent per `(row, event)` … a renewal applies only when the row's `latestEventId` is not already that event's id". That guards an exact replay of the *last* event only: a crash mid-page re-processes a page in which one `dedupeKey` was touched twice, and the older of the two touches then passes an equality test and double-counts `eventCount` against C17. Every ledger application is therefore CAS'd **apply-if-strictly-newer** over the same `(publishedAt, _id)` total order the cursor uses, recorded on the row as `appliedThroughAt`/`appliedThroughId`. This is a strict generalization of D12's rule, not a departure from it, and it is what makes *any* prefix replay a no-op (D3, AC4).
- **Clearing is applied to every row on the cleared `dedupeKey`, not to the clearing event's own matches** — and that is load-bearing, not a detail. A clearing reason is `informational` and normally matches **zero** subscriptions (D4); if clearing were scoped to `matchedSubscriptionIds`, a zero-match recovery would clear nothing and D12's own claim that "recovering `resource` conditions do fall silent on their own" would be false (D4, AC6).
- **One transport adapter ships: Slack** — a separate class from KPR-456's `SlackObligationPoster`, sharing its hardening discipline and its proven nonacceptance code list but **never merged with it** (the two contracts are canon-separate). It registers every posted `ts` through `SlackGateway.registerOutboundTs` (`src/slack/slack-gateway.ts:608`), which is the concrete mechanism by which D10 invariant (b) / C14 is true on this transport: an unregistered ops post lands in a channel the bot listens to, becomes a `WorkItem`, and spawns a turn (D6, AC9).
- **`rejected` schedules no retry, because D6 gives it nothing to schedule from.** The contract's `{ status: "rejected"; reason }` carries no retry hint, so this child ships **no retry ladder**: a `rejected` outcome is recorded on the row and the row's next attempt is its next ordinary nudge. `unknown` never re-sends and never reaches `delivered`; the row stays `pending` and `lastOutcome: unknown` is surfaced as uncertainty (C8). Retry ≠ nudge, and collapsing them is the review trap here.
- **First delivery *is* the first nudge, and the attempt gate is three disjuncts.** A fresh or reopened row is created with `nextNudgeAt = now`, so delivery and nudging are one code path (D8: "a nudge re-delivers the same ledger row through the same transport"). An attempt is gated on `attemptCount === 0 ∨ forceDeliver ∨ a cadence resolved`. The middle disjunct is not decoration: `attemptCount` is monotonic and neither the `cleared → pending` reopen nor a snooze expiry resets it, so a two-disjunct gate would turn a condition that demonstrably came back — and every expired snooze — into permanent silence under the shipped default, contradicting D4, KPR-458 D7 and C10 at once. The safe default that makes merging ahead of KPR-455 honest is therefore **attempted once per occurrence (delivered if the transport accepts), never nudged between occurrences** (D5, D11).
- **A row a tick declines is stalled forward, and the delivery scan is three arms.** `nextNudgeAt` is **never unset** on the cadence-unresolved path — an unset field is invisible to a `$lte` scan under MongoDB's type bracketing and would strand the row permanently — and equally never left at its due value, which would park a monotonically growing, never-TTL'd set (working-state rows carry no `expiresAt`) permanently at the head of the due-scan and silently starve the one guaranteed delivery. Instead the tick writes `stalledReason` and pushes `nextNudgeAt` to a bounded, jittered re-check; and the scan is split so that arm 1 (`attemptCount: 0`) and arm 2 (`forceDeliver`, sparse index) **cannot contain a stalled row at all** and are drawn before arm 3's ordinary nudges. The immunity is an index property, not an ordering convention (D2, D5).
- **Intake's check order is load-bearing.** Resolve handle → require a stable actor id → **idempotence replay check plus a `lastAckAt` monotonicity guard** → `cleared` refusal → `integrity`-dismissal refusal → D7 transition legality → snooze validation and clamp → CAS. The replay check must precede legality, because `dismissed` is not a "from" state in D7's table: a duplicated dismissal callback must return `noop`, not `refused`. The monotonicity guard is there because the replay key records only the *last* act — the same weakness D3 rejects for events — so a delayed duplicate of an **older** act would otherwise pass it, pass legality, and cancel a live snooze (D7, AC8).
- **The snooze clamp always exists, and it is anchored on the server clock.** D12 makes the clamp mandatory, but its maximum is operator data this child ships none of, so intake clamps to `min(requested, anchorAt + min(HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING, registered maximum))` with a contract-side hard ceiling in code. A bound is not a cadence: without one, an unregistered deployment would admit an unbounded snooze, which is the terminal state ruling 2 forbids. **`anchorAt = min(at, now)`, not the caller's `at`** — D6 opens intake to an agent tool call, so an `at`-anchored ceiling is bypassable by anyone who can call the seam; the same anchor governs `stateAt`, `principalAt`, `expiresAt` and `lastAckAt`, so a bad timestamp can neither buy an unbounded pause nor move a per-person retention horizon (D7, D9).
- **⚠ Delegated, all non-blocking:** the ledger TTL as `min(30 d, config.activity.retentionDays)` — the acknowledgement-silence horizon, D9's privacy call realized so the behavioural half always ages before the event log; the hard snooze ceiling (7 d); the `attempts[]` ring cap (5); sweep page sizes and per-phase wall-clock budgets; the stall re-check interval (~5 min) and its jitter; the `evidence`-reference requirement in the `judgment`/`integrity` provenance test; the 60 s subscription reload adopted from KPR-454 D9. **In scope:** the ledger and its indexes, the ingest/cursor handoff, delivery, nudging, snooze expiry, clearing application, intake, the transport interface, one Slack adapter, boot order, shutdown drain. **Out of scope:** match evaluation and the accept path (KPR-454), the inbound acknowledgement edge and every reader, view, CLI and `hive doctor` section (KPR-455), every row of D11's third column, any dashboard, any second transport, any escalation ladder, any de-escalation path.

## Goals and non-goals

**Goals.** Mint and renew one ledger row per `(subscriptionId, dedupeKey)` from the events KPR-454 stamped, without ever re-evaluating a match. Deliver each row once through a transport adapter and nudge it on an operator-registered cadence until it is acknowledged, snoozed, dismissed or cleared. Apply clearing facts after their D8 provenance test. Accept one attributed acknowledgement act at a time through a bounded, idempotent, attribute-or-refuse intake. Never fail, delay or alter a turn. Never publish anything about itself. Be inert until an operator registers subscriptions and cadence.

**Non-goals.** Match evaluation, the accept path, `ops_events`/`ops_subscriptions`/`ops_reasons` creation, and their above-boundary wiring (all KPR-454). The inbound acknowledgement edge, every reader, view, CLI surface, `hive doctor` section and dashboard (KPR-455, and Gate 1 forbids the dashboard). Any subscription row, transport binding, cadence value, nudge floor, snooze maximum, staleness horizon or reason row (D11 column 3, operator). A second transport. A retry ladder, an escalation ladder, a de-escalation or self-healing path, an attempt ceiling, a terminal give-up, or any automatic demotion. Any change to `agent_events`, the scheduler, `activity_log`, delivery routing, KPR-456's obligation path or KPR-457's Beekeeper sender. Any new `hive.yaml` key.

## Design

### D1. What this child owns, and the two collections it creates

D12's exhaustive list, restated as owned scope: (1) ledger row creation and D7-rule-4 renewal driven by the event's stamped `matchedSubscriptionIds`; (2) calling `deliver` on the bound adapter and recording the outcome; (3) the nudge sweep and snooze expiry; (4) applying a clearing event after its D8 provenance test; (5) acknowledgement intake; (6) counting its own faults. **Match evaluation is not here** and the notifier never imports KPR-454's `src/ops/match.ts` — a structural test pins that (AC2).

Two collections are created, indexed and documented in `CLAUDE.md` by this child (the KPR-452 precedent that a collection documented only in a spec is invisible to the next reader):

- **`ops_notifications`** — D1's third contract collection, the acknowledgement ledger. One document per `(subscriptionId, dedupeKey)`.
- **`ops_policy`** — one operator-written document, `_id: "ops"`, holding the D8 cadence table, D5's named cadence profiles, the minimum-nudge floor and the snooze maximum. **Zero rows ship.** It exists because D5 and D11 make those values operator-registered data and D1 named no home for them; the collection is created and indexed here, and its content is the deployment gate.

A third piece of durable state — D12's sweep cursor — is deliberately **not** a collection, per D12's own ruling that it "is not a fourth collection of this contract" and that its location is the implementing child's choice. See D3.

### D2. The `ops_notifications` document

D7's field list is contract and is carried verbatim; the engine-internal additions are enumerated here so a reader can tell contract from mechanism at a glance. Nothing on the row is a new *fact*: every addition is a watermark, a TTL derivation, or a snapshot of a value the accept path already validated against its registry allow-list (C13).

```
{ _id,                                            // ObjectId; its hex string is the D6 acknowledgement handle
  subscriptionId, subscriberId,

  // D7 snapshot, so a reader needs no join
  dedupeKey, producer, reasonId, class, waiting, retry, subject, generation,

  // D7 event linkage
  firstEventId, latestEventId, eventCount, firstSeenAt, lastEventAt,

  // D7 state
  state, stateAt, principal, principalAt, snoozedUntil?,

  // D7 delivery
  attempts[], attemptCount, lastOutcome, deliveryReference?,

  // D7 nudging
  nudgeCount, lastNudgeAt, nextNudgeAt?,

  // ── engine-internal, this child's ──
  appliedThroughAt, appliedThroughId,   // D3: the (publishedAt, _id) watermark that makes every application idempotent
  stateEventId?,                        // D7's table: the event evidencing a clear or a reopen
  latestDetail, latestEvidence,         // snapshot of the latest applied event's allow-listed detail/evidence, so delivery reads no event
  lastAckKey?,                          // D7/intake idempotence: `${actorId}:${act}:${at.toISOString()}` of the last applied act
  lastAckAt?,                           // D7/intake monotonicity: the clock-anchored `at` of the last applied act
  forceDeliver?,                        // D5: sparse `true`; a non-cadence re-delivery is owed (reopen, snooze expiry)
  stalledAt?, stalledReason?,           // D5: sparse; the tick declined to attempt this row and why
  expiresAt? }                          // D9: set only while the row is stopped; the TTL index keys on it
```

**`stalledReason` is a closed union in code, not a string.** `src/ops/notification-types.ts` declares it `"subscription" | "cadence" | "transport"` — one member per decline branch in D5's step list, and nothing else. It is named here because it is the one field on this row shaped like a future error-message sink: a widened one would carry adapter or driver text onto the ledger, which C13 forbids, and would still pass AC12's negative test, whose substrings are credential- and path-shaped rather than prose-shaped. The union is what makes the three gauges' `stalledReason` index a bounded three-valued key rather than an open one.

`state` is D7's closed six: `pending` · `delivered` · `seen` · `dismissed` · `snoozed` · `cleared`. `principal` is a stable actor id for the three acknowledgement states and the **reserved system principal** — a fixed non-human id no person or agent may hold — for every unattended transition (D7 rule 5, D9). This spec fixes that reserved id as the constant `OPS_SYSTEM_PRINCIPAL = "system:ops"`, and fixes its **shape** rather than leaving it to prose, because "unambiguously non-human" is a judgment a later reader re-makes and this repo's canon is code-enforcement: the embedded colon is a character neither namespace that supplies a real `actorId` is expected to produce — an `agent_definitions` id is lowercase-with-hyphens and a Slack user id is `U`/`W` plus uppercase alphanumerics — so a colliding actor id is not reachable through either convention. **That is a convention claim, not a checked one, and the distinction matters**: the agent-id shape is documented (CLAUDE.md, Conventions) and described in the admin tool's schema (`src/admin/admin-mcp-server.ts:519` carries it as a zod `.describe()` string) but is **not** regex-enforced anywhere, so a hand-inserted `agent_definitions` document could in principle hold a colon. What makes the property safe is therefore the *direction* of the failure rather than the impossibility of the input: an actor presenting the reserved id is **refused** by D7 step 2, never impersonated, so a collision costs that actor their acknowledgement and costs the ledger nothing. A test pins the constant against both shapes — which asserts a property of the constant, not of any real id — and intake's refusal is what carries C9.

**`attempts[]` is a ring, `attemptCount` is the total.** D7 requires a contract-fixed cap chosen by the implementing child: **5**. Each entry is `{ at, outcome: "accepted" | "rejected" | "unknown", reason?, adapterId }` — never a rendered string, never a raw transport error (C13, and the KPR-457 canon that raw diagnostics stay out of allow-listed fields). `nudgeCount` increments on an attempt made when `attemptCount > 0` at attempt time, so the first delivery is an attempt but not a nudge even though it travels the nudge path (D5).

**Indexes**, created in `init()` on the `obligations/store.ts` shape, each individually contained and counted (D10):

| Collection | Index | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ops_notifications` | `{ subscriptionId: 1, dedupeKey: 1 }` **unique** | Row identity. This is what makes the D4 upsert idempotent under a concurrent or restarted sweep — the duplicate-key error *is* the race detector, not a nuisance. |
| `ops_notifications` | `{ dedupeKey: 1 }` | Clearing application, which fans out across every subscription holding the key and therefore cannot use the compound index above. |
| `ops_notifications` | `{ state: 1, attemptCount: 1, nextNudgeAt: 1 }` | **Arm 1** of the delivery scan — the guaranteed first delivery (D5). Equality on `attemptCount: 0` means an already-attempted row is not in this index's scanned prefix **at all**, which is what makes arm 1 structurally immune to a backlog of stalled rows rather than merely ordered ahead of one. |
| `ops_notifications` | `{ forceDeliver: 1, nextNudgeAt: 1 }` **sparse** | **Arm 2** — the two non-cadence re-delivery triggers (D5). Sparse, so only rows actually owed one are in it. The absent `state` key is **not** an omission: no non-working row carries a `nextNudgeAt` at all (D5's scoping of the never-unset rule), so this index's range holds only `pending`/`delivered` rows by type bracketing rather than by a residual filter. |
| `ops_notifications` | `{ state: 1, nextNudgeAt: 1 }` | **Arm 3** — ordinary cadence nudges. |
| `ops_notifications` | `{ stalledReason: 1, state: 1 }` **sparse** | The three standing-depth stall gauges, as a bounded saturating count (D5). Sparse on the leading field, so it holds only rows a tick actually declined; `state` is in the index rather than a residual filter because a saturating `limit` over a residual filter does not saturate — it scans until it has found `limit` matches. |
| `ops_notifications` | `{ lastOutcome: 1, state: 1 }` **sparse** | The `rowsUnknownOutcome` gauge, as a bounded saturating count (D5). Sparse on the leading field, so only rows that have actually been attempted are in it; `state` is in the index for the same saturation reason as the row above. |
| `ops_notifications` | `{ state: 1, snoozedUntil: 1 }` | The snooze-expiry scan. |
| `ops_notifications` | `{ expiresAt: 1 }`, `expireAfterSeconds: 0` | D9's state-dependent retention. A TTL on `stateAt` would delete *working* rows; the conditional lives in the field, not the index. |
| `ops_policy` | none beyond `_id` | One document. |

Unlike KPR-454's, **the unique index here carries a correctness role** — it is the only thing preventing two rows for one `(subscriptionId, dedupeKey)` under a racing insert — so it sits on the `MeetingWorkerPool.ensureIndexes()` side of the engine's own line rather than the `MeetingScribe` side. That does **not** make it boot-fatal (D10): a failure to create it leaves the publisher's own posture intact and the notifier **unstarted**, which is this child's defined safe state. The remaining indexes are performance or housekeeping and are contained individually.

### D3. The ingest handoff: cursor, ordering, and idempotence

**The sweep reads `matchedSubscriptionIds` and never re-evaluates matching** (D12, C2). Re-evaluation would let a subscription registered after accept acquire a past event, contradicting D5, and would make the stored count a lie.

**The cursor lives in `db.telemetry`, `kind: "ops_sweep_cursor"`**, holding `{ publishedAt, eventId }` — the position in `ops_events`'s `(publishedAt, _id)` total order through which every ledger application is durable. It is a cursor and not a record: it holds no facts, nothing but the sweep reads it, and it is not a collection of this contract. `db.telemetry` is chosen over a dedicated collection because it is already engine-written, carries no TTL, and is upserted per `kind` — and, critically, because losing it is nearly free under the initialization rule below. The heartbeat is a **separate** document (`kind: "ops_notifier_stats"`) so that overwriting stats can never clobber the cursor.

**A missing cursor initializes to the server clock, never to the beginning of the log**, with a counted warning. This is the cold-start rule and it is deliberate in both directions. Forward: KPR-454 ships ahead of this child and accumulates events for however long the epic takes, and a notifier that woke up and delivered weeks of backlog would be exactly the flood this epic exists to remove — worse, it would re-mint rows for conditions whose ledger rows had already aged out under D9, resurrecting long-dead acknowledgements. Backward: the cost of the rule is that events published while no cursor existed are never notified, which for a cursor lost at time *T* and re-initialized on the next tick is bounded by one sweep interval and is countable in the heartbeat's `eventsBehind`.

**Advance only after the writes are durable.** Each tick reads a bounded page of `ops_events` strictly after the cursor, sorted `{ publishedAt: 1, _id: 1 }` (the index KPR-454 creates for exactly this), applies each event **strictly in order**, and writes the cursor to the last fully-applied event's position with majority write concern only after the page's writes have been acknowledged. A crash between the two re-processes the page rather than skipping it (D12), which is safe because of the next rule. **Every ledger write carries the same `{ w: "majority", wtimeoutMS: 5000 }` KPR-456 uses** (`src/obligations/store.ts:20`, `receipts.ts:70`, `sweeper.ts:374`): the durability ordering this paragraph promises is only nominal if the ledger upserts run at the driver default and a majority-durable cursor can then outlive the writes it claims to trail. The same concern is used on intake's CAS, for the same reason at the row level. Ordering is what makes D12's guarantee that "a clearing event can never be applied before the condition event it clears" true by construction rather than by luck; an out-of-order application within a page would break it.

**Idempotence is apply-if-strictly-newer, which is a strengthening of D12's stated rule and the reason it is stated here.** D12 offers `latestEventId` equality — "a renewal applies only when the row's `latestEventId` is not already that event's id". That is sufficient for a replay of exactly one event and insufficient for the crash D12 itself describes: re-processing a page in which one `dedupeKey` appears twice replays the *older* touch against a row whose `latestEventId` names the newer one, the equality test passes, and `eventCount` increments a second time — a direct C17 failure. Every application therefore CAS's on

```
appliedThroughAt < e.publishedAt  ∨  (appliedThroughAt = e.publishedAt ∧ appliedThroughId < e._id)
```

and sets `appliedThroughAt`/`appliedThroughId` to the applied event's position in the same write. Consequences: replaying any prefix of the log is a total no-op; an out-of-order event is discarded rather than mis-applied; `eventCount` counts distinct events, never sweep attempts (C17); and a re-processed clearing event cannot re-clear a row that has since been reopened and re-cleared by a newer fact. The `(publishedAt, _id)` comparison is the same total order KPR-454's epoch resolver uses and rests on the same property — the publisher's serial drainer is the sole `_id` minter, so insertion order is publish order.

**That property is a constraint on the next producer, not an inherited fact — stated here so it is not a surprise.** "Insertion order is publish order" is true of KPR-454 *alone*, and it is what makes this section's no-skip claim true. `publishedAt` is stamped at accept (KPR-454 D9 step 7) and the insert lands later, so a **second concurrent producer** into `ops_events` could insert an event whose `publishedAt` is already behind an advanced cursor; that event would never be notified — silently, uncounted, and invisible to `eventsBehind`, since it is not *behind* the cursor but *under* it. KPR-458 forecasts exactly that producer (D10: KPR-456's sweep is "later, a natural *producer* into this log"; D2: a producer that does not exist yet "must be addable as data"). This is the same class of residual as the TTL-outrun gap below and is named on the same terms. It is **not** solved here — no second producer exists — but it is a **precondition on the child that adds one**: either that producer serializes its inserts through the same drainer, or the cursor gains a safety lag (advance only through events older than a bound exceeding the widest accept→insert window), or the skip is measured and accepted. A second producer added without choosing one of those three silently loses events.

**Page sizes and per-phase budgets are delegated; the behaviour on breach is fixed.** A tick ingests successive pages until a page is short or a per-tick event budget is reached; leftover work is picked up on the next tick and is visible as `eventsBehind` in the heartbeat. Nothing is dropped and nothing is skipped.

**One honest gap, named rather than papered over.** If the sweep falls so far behind that events reach `ops_events`'s TTL horizon before it reaches them, those events are deleted and the cursor advances past a gap. The heartbeat is the only signal, and D5 pins the shape it takes: `oldestUnappliedAt` compared against the event TTL is the direct measure a reader (KPR-455) should surface — an age against an age — with the bounded `eventsBehind` beside it as the load signal rather than as the gap's proxy. No mechanism prevents it, because the alternative — holding events past their retention — would contradict D9.

### D4. Row creation, renewal, and clearing application

For each event `e` in log order, two independent applications, in this order:

**(a) Clearing, when `e.clears` is present.** Every row whose `dedupeKey` equals `e.clears` — **across all subscriptions, independent of `e.matchedSubscriptionIds`**. This scope is not an optimization and reversing it breaks the contract: a clearing reason is `informational` and normally matches zero subscriptions (D4's example table and KPR-454's own `tool-recovered` row), so a match-scoped clearing would clear nothing, every `resource` condition would nudge forever, and D12's assertion that "recovering `resource` conditions do fall silent on their own — D4's enable gate guarantees every `resource` reason has a registered clearing reason" would be false.

The **provenance test is decided by the cleared row's snapshotted `class`** (D8, using D3's table), and it is restricted to what the envelope makes checkable — this component judges nothing:

| cleared row's `class` | test |
| --- | --- |
| `resource` | `e.producer === row.producer` — D3's "a clearing event from the same producer asserting the recorded predicate now holds". |
| `judgment` | same producer **and** `e.evidence.length >= 1` — D3's "an event recording a named human ruling"; the ruling's record is the reference. ⚠ delegated. |
| `integrity` | same producer **and** `e.evidence.length >= 1` — D3's "only a clearing event carrying the underlying authoritative evidence", read literally. ⚠ delegated. |
| `informational` | never clears (D3: "no clearing act"). |

**The same-producer clause is not defence-in-depth; it closes a case publish-time validation genuinely misses.** D4 requires `clearsReasonIds` to name reasons of the *same* producer and C19 enforces that at publish — but the enforcement is over the **reasonId component only**: KPR-454's accept path computes `reasonIdOfDedupeKey(input.clears)` and tests its membership in the publishing row's `clearsReasonIds` (`kpr-454-plan-3-publisher.md:630-635`). `dedupeKey` is `producer:subjectKind:subjectId:reasonId:generation` (D2), so the **producer component of the `clears` key is never constrained**: a `clears` naming a *different* producer's dedupeKey whose reasonId happens to collide with a registered clearer passes publish intact. Nothing upstream catches it, and the consequence is one producer silently clearing another's condition — precisely the cross-producer authority C19 exists to deny. This clause is the only check that does catch it, it costs one string comparison the notifier already has both sides of, and AC6 pins it so the property is checked rather than incidental. A clearing event that fails its test **is already stored** (the publisher stored it; it is a fact that someone tried) and **does not transition the row**; the notifier counts `clearRefused` and moves on. A clearing event naming a `dedupeKey` with no row is a counted no-op — nobody was listening, which D8 says is answered from the log rather than from the ledger.

On a passing test the row transitions to `cleared` under the system principal with `stateEventId = e._id`, `expiresAt` set (D9), and `nextNudgeAt` unset.

**The clearing arm deliberately leaves `forceDeliver` and `stalledAt`/`stalledReason` alone, and there is no fourth write arm for them.** A row cleared while it was owed a re-delivery, or while it was stalled, keeps both markers until it either TTLs out or is reopened. Both are **provably inert** rather than merely harmless: `nextNudgeAt` is unset on a `cleared` row, so no delivery arm's index range contains it (D5's scoping) and a retained `forceDeliver` can never cause an attempt; and every stall gauge's index carries `state ∈ {pending, delivered}`, so a retained `stalledReason` is not counted. A reopen (the renewal table in (b)) re-sets `forceDeliver: true` and unsets the stall markers explicitly, so nothing is inherited across the boundary either. This is stated once, with the reasoning, because the next reader will otherwise re-derive it — or, worse, add a clearing-arm unset that costs a write and buys nothing.

**The fan-out is per-row CAS'd writes, never an `updateMany` — and that is not in tension with the fan-out itself.** The apply-if-newer watermark (D3) is a *per-row* precondition: two rows on the same `dedupeKey` can sit at different `appliedThrough*` positions, because they were minted by different events and may have been renewed a different number of times. A bulk update cannot express "apply only where this row's watermark is older", so the clearing phase iterates the `{ dedupeKey: 1 }` index and issues one conditional `updateOne` per row. This is stated because the two facts read as contradictory at a glance and an implementer who reaches for `updateMany` here will hit AC8's "no code path issues an `updateMany` against `ops_notifications`" assertion and read the failing test as a harness bug rather than as the design. The same rule applies to the reopen arm in (b).

**`stateAt` on this and every other system transition is the tick's `now`, not the event's `publishedAt`.** The three system transitions are the clear here, the snooze expiry (D5) and the delivery state change (D5 step 5), and all three take the tick's clock. `expiresAt` derives from `stateAt` (D9), so a backlogged sweep applying a clearing event published hours ago would otherwise set a retention horizon measured from that older instant and could TTL a row very shortly after — or, on a badly behind sweep, immediately — deleting it before any reader saw it close. The event's own instant is not lost: it is on `lastEventAt` and `stateEventId` already. (Intake is the deliberate exception: its `stateAt` is `anchorAt`, the acknowledger's clock-anchored act instant — D7.)

**A `dismissed` row does clear** — D7's table admits "any state → `cleared`", and that is correct: a dismissal stops nudging a subscriber, and a later clearing fact records that the condition genuinely resolved. **A `cleared` row is a no-op** by the D3 watermark.

**Epoch interaction, stated because it is the question a reviewer asks first.** The notifier is generation-blind by construction. `generation` is a component of `dedupeKey` (D2's formula), so a new epoch is a different key and therefore a different row: an advanced generation mints a fresh `pending` row beside the closed one and can never touch it. A clearing event carries an exact `dedupeKey` in `clears`, so it can only ever reach its own epoch's row. KPR-454 D8's named residual — a faulted epoch resolve attaching an event to a stale epoch's key — lands here as a clearing fact applied to a stale row that was already stale: a bounded mis-attachment of a notification, never a lost fact, exactly as that spec states.

**(b) Renewal or creation, for `e`'s own `dedupeKey`, across `e.matchedSubscriptionIds`.** Each stamped id is resolved against the loaded subscription map (D5). A stamped subscription that is absent, disabled, or whose target fails `validateTarget` yields **no row**, and the event's stored `matchedSubscriptions` count still stands — D12's exact rule: the count was honest for the set that existed at accept and was never a delivery promise. A clearing event also mints or renews its **own** rows here for any subscriber that deliberately subscribed to recoveries (D4); it never renews the cleared condition's row, which transitions by (a) alone.

The write is **two steps, not an aggregation-pipeline update**, because the `(subscriptionId, dedupeKey)` unique index must remain the race detector and the repo's test double supports neither pipeline updates nor an upsert whose filter carries operators (`fake-db.ts:279-302`):

1. `updateOne({ subscriptionId, dedupeKey, <apply-if-newer CAS> }, { $set: renewal, $inc: { eventCount: 1 }, ... })` — **no `upsert`**. `matchedCount === 1` ⇒ applied. `matchedCount === 0` ⇒ either the row does not exist, or it exists and the event is not newer.
2. On `matchedCount === 0`, `insertOne` a fresh row. A duplicate-key error means the row existed and the event was not newer — the second branch of step 1's ambiguity — and is a **no-op, not a fault**: caught, counted as `renewalStale`, never surfaced as an error.

Fresh rows are created `state: "pending"`, `principal: OPS_SYSTEM_PRINCIPAL`, `firstEventId`/`latestEventId` = `e._id`, `eventCount: 1`, `attemptCount: 0`, `nudgeCount: 0`, `nextNudgeAt: now` (D5), `appliedThrough*` at `e`'s position.

Renewal semantics are D7 rule 4 verbatim, applied against the existing state:

| row state | renewal effect |
| --- | --- |
| `pending`, `delivered` | `latestEventId`/`lastEventAt`/`eventCount`/`latestDetail`/`latestEvidence` advance. `nextNudgeAt` is **not** reset — a repeat must not restart the cadence clock, or a flapping producer nudges on its own schedule instead of the operator's. |
| `seen`, `dismissed` | advance and send nothing new. |
| `snoozed` | advance; the row still resumes on its own at `snoozedUntil`. |
| `cleared` | **reopen** to `pending` under the system principal with `stateEventId = e._id`, `nextNudgeAt = now`, **`forceDeliver: true`**, `expiresAt` unset, `stalledAt`/`stalledReason` unset. The condition demonstrably came back while the producer did not, or could not, advance its epoch, and the design errs toward a duplicate rather than a gap. **`forceDeliver` is what makes that sentence true under the shipped default** — `attemptCount` is monotonic and is *not* reset by a reopen, so without it a reopened row would fail an `attemptCount === 0 ∨ cadence` gate and a condition that demonstrably came back would never be delivered at all (D5). |

### D5. Cadence, the nudge sweep, and snooze expiry

**Cadence comes from the contract's data, never from this component.** The `ops_policy` document (D1), read once per tick — one small `findOne`, so it is always fresh and needs no reload timer. **The tick retains the document it read**, and that retained copy — not a second read — is what intake's snooze clamp consults (D7 step 7); this spec picks that side of the choice explicitly, because D7 bounds intake at "one indexed read plus one CAS" and a second `findOne` would break that bound for no gain. Staleness is at most one 30 s interval and is harmless against a value whose only job is to *tighten* a hard ceiling the code already carries. **The cold path is intake running before the first tick has completed** — legitimate, since intake is available from `init()` (D10) while the sweep starts later — and it resolves to `HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING`, the same answer as an unregistered `ops_policy`:

```
{ _id: "ops",
  cadence?: { "<class>:<retry>": <intervalMs> },   // D8's table, keyed exactly as D8 keys it; at most 8 entries
  profiles?: { "<name>": <intervalMs> },           // D5's named cadence profiles
  minNudgeIntervalMs?: number,                     // D5's operator-set floor
  maxSnoozeMs?: number }                           // D8/D12's snooze maximum
```

**A failed `ops_policy` read retains the previous copy, counts, and writes no stall markers** — the subscription map's posture (D6: "a fault leaves the previous set in place and counts, never empties it"), mirrored deliberately rather than by coincidence, because the asymmetry would be destructive rather than merely inconsistent. `findOne → null` (nothing registered) and `findOne → throw` (a transient storage fault) are distinguishable at the driver, so only the first resolves as "no policy" and this costs nothing. Resolving a throw as absent would make **every working row cadence-unresolved on that tick**: the tick would write `stalledAt`/`stalledReason: "cadence"` across the whole set and push every `nextNudgeAt` forward by `STALL_RECHECK_MS`, turning a two-second Mongo blip into a full re-check interval of silence for every subscriber and a spike in `rowsCadenceUnresolved` that reflects the blip rather than the deployment. On a failed read the tick therefore uses the copy it retained from an earlier tick; if it has none — the first tick of a process — the **delivery phase is skipped entirely for that tick**, counted `policyReadFaults`, with that phase's `ok` flag false so the heartbeat reads `degraded`. No row is marked, nothing is pushed forward, and the next tick resolves normally.

Resolution for one row, and **the anti-merge rule is enforced rather than documented**:

- If the row's subscription names a `cadenceProfile`, the interval is `profiles[name]` and **the `(class, retry)` table is not consulted at all** — D5's "substitutes that profile wholesale", with no field-by-field merge, no per-attribute override, and no inheritance. An **unknown** profile name therefore does **not** fall back to the table: the row resolves no cadence, is counted `cadenceUnresolved`, and does not nudge. A fallback would be precisely the two-tables-and-an-implicit-precedence rules engine D5 refuses.
- Otherwise the interval is `cadence["<row.class>:<row.retry>"]`.
- Either way the interval is clamped up to `minNudgeIntervalMs` when set, and a profile below the floor is clamped up with a **warn-once per process, memoized on `(profileName, intervalMs)`** — D5 says "loudly, at registration", and registration here is a Mongo write by an operator that no code path observes, so load time is the honest substitution. **Per *process*, not per load, and the difference is the whole point:** the policy document is read on **every 30 s tick**, so a "warn-once per load" rule would emit one line per bad profile every 30 s forever — a permanent log loop in exactly the steady state a mis-registered profile produces, and the opposite of the repo's convention everywhere else (the `modelToTier` family heuristic, the `:effort` coercions and the `ENABLE_TOOL_SEARCH` ambient-override notice are all warn-once per process, memoized on the offending key). Keying on the *value* as well as the name means a corrected-then-re-broken profile warns again, which is the one case a bare name key would swallow. The **standing** depth is carried by the `rowsCadenceUnresolved` gauge, which is what that gauge is for.
- **No resolvable interval ⇒ the row is delivered once and never *nudged* — but "delivered once, ever" is not the property, and the difference is stated below.** This is the deployment gate, not a failure: D11 makes cadence values operator-registered data that this child ships none of, and KPR-456 canon forbids an engineering child supplying a default policy dependency. **How that is realized is load-bearing and is fixed here, because both obvious implementations are silently wrong.** Unsetting `nextNudgeAt` would make the row invisible to the due-scan forever: MongoDB comparison operators are type-bracketed, so a document with no `nextNudgeAt` never matches `{ nextNudgeAt: { $lte: now } }` — and the repo's own double implements exactly that bracketing (`src/obligations/testing/fake-db.ts:40-41`, `case "$lte": return actual !== undefined && actual <= value`). Every row delivered once under no resolvable cadence would be stranded permanently, `rowsCadenceUnresolved` could not re-observe it, and "resumes after a cadence is registered" would be false. **Leaving `nextNudgeAt` at its already-due value** fixes the stranding and creates a second defect: the row then sits permanently at the head of the due-scan (see "the stall", below). The rule is therefore neither — it is *unset never, and pushed forward on a bounded re-check*.

**The never-unset rule is scoped to the working states, and that scoping is what keeps the arms clean.** `nextNudgeAt` is never unset **while a row is `pending` or `delivered`** — that is the cadence-unresolved path above, and the permanent stranding it prevents. Every transition **out** of a working state unsets it: `seen` and `dismissed` (D7 step 8), `snoozed` (D7 step 8; the expiry phase below re-sets it to `now` on the way back in), and `cleared` (D4). The consequence is a type-bracketing property rather than a convention: **no non-working row carries a `nextNudgeAt` at all**, so none of the three delivery arms' index ranges contains one. That is what lets arm 2's index be `{ forceDeliver: 1, nextNudgeAt: 1 }` with no `state` key. Read the other way, the scoping is load-bearing rather than tidy: a `snoozed` row can legitimately carry `forceDeliver: true` — a reopen or a snooze expiry sets it, and an intake `snoozed` does not clear it, which is reachable whenever an acknowledgement lands between the tick that marked the row and the tick that would have delivered it — so under a "retain `nextNudgeAt` on the `snoozed` arm" reading that row would sit in arm 2's index range with a due value, and `state ∈ {pending, delivered}` would become a **residual filter** over arm 2 under the same saturating phase budget. That is verbatim the defect the `{ stalledReason: 1, state: 1 }` index note in D2 rejects for the gauges. The alternative fix — adding `state` to arm 2's index — is strictly more machinery for the same property, so the unset is taken.

**First delivery is the first nudge, and the attempt gate is three disjuncts, not two.** A fresh or reopened row carries `nextNudgeAt = now`, so first delivery and nudging are the same due-scan and there is exactly one delivery code path (D8: "A nudge re-delivers the same ledger row through the same transport; it never creates a second row"). `seen`, `dismissed` and `cleared` are not nudge-eligible; `snoozed` is suppressed until expiry. A due row is attempted iff

```
attemptCount === 0  ∨  forceDeliver === true  ∨  a cadence resolved for it
```

— the first disjunct is the one guaranteed delivery, the third is every nudge, and the second is **the two re-delivery triggers that are neither**.

**Why the second disjunct exists, stated plainly because its absence was a silent gap.** `attemptCount` is monotonic: nothing resets it, and neither the `cleared → pending` reopen (D4) nor snooze expiry (below) touches it. Under a two-disjunct gate and the shipped default (no `ops_policy`), a condition that was cleared and **demonstrably came back** would set `nextNudgeAt = now` and then never be delivered at all — flatly contradicting D4's own "the design errs toward a duplicate rather than a gap" and KPR-458 D7's "a reopened `cleared` row is `pending`, so its next delivery goes through the ordinary attempt path rather than a special case". The same gate would turn an expired snooze into permanent silence for a row that *was* being delivered, against C10's "`snoozed` is the only pause **and it expires** … no act can pause a row indefinitely". So a sparse `forceDeliver: true` is written at exactly those two arms — the reopen and the snooze expiry — and is cleared by the attempt that consumes it (and by an intake `seen`/`dismissed`, for the same reason `nextNudgeAt` is cleared there). A skip never clears it: a forced row that is stalled on an unresolved subscription or an unbound adapter is still owed its delivery when that clears.

The honest property under the shipped default is therefore: **a condition is *attempted* once per occurrence — its first appearance, each recurrence after a clear, and each expiry of a snooze — and is never nudged between them; it is *delivered* on that attempt if the transport accepts.** The word is `attempted`, not `delivered`, and the gap is real rather than pedantic: step 5 consumes `forceDeliver` and increments `attemptCount` on **every** outcome, so under the shipped default a first attempt that comes back `rejected` (a `refused-rate-limit`, say) or `unknown` means that occurrence is never delivered at all, with no re-attempt until the row's next occurrence. That is the deliberate consequence of shipping no retry ladder (D5 step 5) and is stated here because the shorter phrase is repeated in the TL;DR, D11, AC11 and `CLAUDE.md`, where an operator reads it.

**The stall: a row a tick declines is pushed forward, not left at the head of the queue.** Steps 1–3 below can each decline to attempt a due row — unresolved subscription, unresolved cadence, unbound adapter. A declined row is **not** left untouched: the tick writes `stalledAt = now`, `stalledReason`, and `nextNudgeAt = now + STALL_RECHECK_MS ± jitter` (⚠ delegated; ~5 minutes and a small jitter fraction). Three reasons, and the second is the one that makes this mandatory rather than tidy:

- *Unset is fatal* (type bracketing, above), so the field must stay set — the round-1 pin is preserved.
- *Left-at-due is unbounded.* A cadence-unresolved row sits in `pending`/`delivered`, which are **working** states, so `expiresAt` is unset and it **never TTLs** (D9). Under the shipped default every row is cadence-unresolved, so the set grows monotonically for the life of the deployment, and on `{ state: 1, nextNudgeAt: 1 }` those rows carry the *oldest* `nextNudgeAt` and so sort **ahead of every freshly-minted row**. Once that prefix exceeds the delivery phase's wall-clock budget, the `attemptCount === 0` first delivery — the one guaranteed delivery the whole merge-ahead argument rests on — is starved, and the failure is invisible: the row stays `pending`, nothing errors. Pushing a declined row forward keeps it out of the due set between re-checks; jitter keeps a cohort that stalled on the same tick from re-arriving as one herd.
- *Bounded re-check preserves the lever.* The row is re-observed at most once per `STALL_RECHECK_MS`, so D11's levers stay one-way-free with a stated latency rather than "on the first tick".

`STALL_RECHECK_MS` is a **re-evaluation backoff, not a delivery cadence**: it never decides whether, to whom, or how often anything is delivered; it bounds only how soon the engine re-evaluates a decision it already declined. A row whose cadence is still unresolved at re-check is simply stalled again. It is therefore not the engine default policy KPR-456 canon forbids an engineering child from supplying — the same class as the 30 s sweep interval and the 60 s subscription reload. **What it is not is "nothing is ever delivered on it", which an earlier draft claimed and which is false:** an `attemptCount === 0` row stalled on `subscription` or `transport` is *first delivered* on a re-check tick, once the subscription is re-enabled or the adapter is bound. That is the point of re-checking; the accurate claim is the narrower one about what the constant decides, not about what happens on the tick it schedules.

**The scan is three ordered arms, and arms 1 and 2 are structurally immune to the stall set.** The stall alone bounds the *steady-state* cost; it does not by itself guarantee that a re-check tick's arriving cohort cannot crowd out a new row on that tick. That guarantee is the arm split (D2's index table), served in order under one shared phase budget:

| arm | query (all `state ∈ {pending, delivered}`, all sorted `{ nextNudgeAt: 1 }`) | why it cannot be starved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `{ attemptCount: 0, nextNudgeAt: { $lte: now } }` | Equality on `attemptCount: 0` — an already-attempted row is not in `{ state, attemptCount, nextNudgeAt }`'s scanned prefix at all. |
| 2 | `{ forceDeliver: true, nextNudgeAt: { $lte: now } }` | The index is **sparse**; only rows actually owed a re-delivery are in it. |
| 3 | `{ nextNudgeAt: { $lte: now } }` | Ordinary nudges. This is the arm a stall cohort lands in, and it is drawn last. |

A row matching more than one arm is attempted once per tick (arm 1 and arm 2 both imply the gate passes; the tick tracks the row ids it has already attempted). Round 2 offered two remedies — a query-level exclusion resolving the deliverable `(class, retry)`/profile key set into the filter, or an explicit `attemptCount: 0`-first ordering. **The exclusion is declined and the ordering is taken, strengthened into an index property**, for two reasons: cadence resolution depends on the row's *subscription's* `cadenceProfile`, which is not a field on the row, so an exclusion predicate would have to carry an unbounded `$in` of subscription ids beside the ≤ 8 `(class, retry)` pairs; and a `$or` of that shape would in any case be applied as a residual filter over `{ state: 1, nextNudgeAt: 1 }`, i.e. it would still walk the stall prefix. The arm split moves the guarantee into the index, where a query planner cannot lose it.

**One attempt, per row, per tick:**

1. Resolve the subscription from the loaded map; unresolved ⇒ **stall** (`stalledReason: "subscription"`), counted `subscriptionUnresolved` (the operator kill switch, D11).
2. **Resolve the cadence and apply the attempt gate.** Gate fails — i.e. `attemptCount > 0`, no `forceDeliver`, and no cadence — ⇒ **stall** (`stalledReason: "cadence"`), counted `cadenceUnresolved`. This step precedes the adapter and the inter-attempt spacing below; it is a filter pass over the rows arm 3 returned, **and it does consume the delivery phase's wall-clock budget** like every other row the phase touches. (An earlier draft claimed it "costs a filter pass rather than the delivery phase's budget" — that claim was false against this phase's own stated stop condition, and the stall plus the arm split are what actually bound the cost.)
3. Resolve the adapter by `subscription.transport.adapterId`; unbound ⇒ **stall** (`stalledReason: "transport"`), counted `transportUnbound`.
4. Build the `NotificationView` (D6) and `await adapter.deliver(view)`, bounded by **the adapter's own deadline, not by the sweep's patience** (D12).
5. Record the outcome: push onto the `attempts[]` ring, `$inc attemptCount`, `$inc nudgeCount` when the row had already been attempted, set `lastNudgeAt`, set `lastOutcome`, and **unset `forceDeliver`, `stalledAt` and `stalledReason`** — the attempt is what consumes the forced re-delivery, on every outcome alike, since a `rejected` or `unknown` attempt was still made and the trigger has been served.
   - `accepted` ⇒ `state: "delivered"` (from `pending`) under the system principal, `deliveryReference` set, `nextNudgeAt = now + interval`.
   - `rejected` ⇒ state unchanged, `nextNudgeAt = now + interval`. **No retry ladder ships**, and the contract is what decides that: D6's `{ status: "rejected"; reason }` carries no retry hint, so this component has nothing to schedule a retry from and inventing a backoff schedule would be cadence policy by another name. C8 permits an automatic retry on `rejected`; it does not require one, and the row's next ordinary nudge *is* the retry, reusing the same row and incrementing the same counters exactly as D6 requires.
   - `unknown` ⇒ state unchanged (it never reaches `delivered`), `nextNudgeAt = now + interval`, and `lastOutcome` is what every reader must surface as **uncertainty, never as notified** (C8). This is KPR-456's rule preserved, not superseded: nothing is re-sent on the strength of an `unknown`; the later nudge is the condition's ordinary cadence, not a resend of the attempt.
   - An adapter that **throws** rather than returning an outcome is contained and recorded as `unknown` / `transport-fault`, counted, never escaping the tick.

   `nextNudgeAt = now + interval` is written on all three outcomes alike — the outcome never changes the schedule, only the state. On an attempt made with **no** interval (the first and second disjuncts under an unregistered cadence), `nextNudgeAt = now + STALL_RECHECK_MS ± jitter` and `stalledAt`/`stalledReason: "cadence"` are written in the same update instead: the row has taken the delivery it was owed and is now an ordinary cadence-unresolved row, on the same footing as one step 2 declined.

**Delivery is spaced and budgeted.** Attempts within a tick are made one at a time with a minimum start spacing (the shape KPR-457 proved, adopted as a shape and not as its contract) and the phase stops on a wall-clock budget, deferring the remainder to the next tick with the backlog visible in the heartbeat. Values are ⚠ delegated; the behaviour on breach is fixed. **The budget is wall-clock and is counted over every row the phase touches, attempted or declined** — which is precisely why the stall and the arm split above exist rather than a bare cap on the scan.

**Snooze expiry runs before delivery within a tick**, so a row whose snooze expires on this tick is re-delivered on this tick rather than waiting for the next one; the phase order is fixed here because either order is defensible and an unstated one becomes an accidental extra interval of silence for a row the acknowledger explicitly asked to be reminded about. It is its own bounded phase: `{ state: "snoozed", snoozedUntil: { $lte: now } }` ⇒ **`delivered` if the row has a recorded `accepted` outcome, else `pending`** (D7's table verbatim), under the system principal, with `nextNudgeAt = now`, **`forceDeliver: true`** and `snoozedUntil` unset. D7 is explicit that an expired row is not "eligible" but *is* nudge-eligible by that fact, so no special case exists downstream — and `forceDeliver` is what makes that true under the shipped default, since the row's `attemptCount` is by construction greater than zero (it was being delivered when it was snoozed) and C10's "`snoozed` … expires" would otherwise resolve to permanent silence.

**Sweep cadence, boundedness and non-overlap.** A 30 s interval, the KPR-456 precedent (`sweeper.ts:21`), with `sweepOnce()`'s single in-flight promise (`:106-117`) so ticks never overlap. Each phase is independently contained: a fault in one row is caught, counted, and does not abort the tick or block the next one (D12 containment (b)). The tick checks its stopped latch between rows and phases so shutdown drains promptly.

**Honesty when behind.** Every tick upserts `db.telemetry` `kind: "ops_notifier_stats"`: `lastSuccessfulSweep`, `cursorAt`, `eventsBehind`, `oldestUnappliedAt`, `rowsPending`, `rowsNudgeDue`, `rowsSnoozed`, `rowsUnknownOutcome`, `rowsSubscriptionUnresolved`, `rowsTransportUnbound`, `rowsCadenceUnresolved`, the fault counters, and a `state` of `ok` | `backlog` | `degraded` on the KPR-456 pattern (all phases succeeded and no backlog ⇒ `ok`; all succeeded with backlog ⇒ `backlog`; any phase failed ⇒ `degraded`). This is the measurement surface KPR-455 reads. **No `hive doctor` section ships here** — the reader is KPR-455's charter.

**`eventsBehind` is pinned to a bounded form rather than delegated**, because it is the only signal for the TTL-outrun gap and the obvious implementation — a `countDocuments` over every `ops_events` document past the cursor — is unbounded precisely when the sweep is most behind, i.e. exactly when it must not add load. A tick that drained the log reports `0` and `oldestUnappliedAt: null` with no extra read. A tick that stopped on its budget reports (i) `oldestUnappliedAt`, the `publishedAt` of the first un-applied document, and (ii) `eventsBehind` from a **`countDocuments` carrying a `limit`** (⚠ value delegated), so the count saturates at that limit and is read as "at least N" rather than as an exact depth.

**`oldestUnappliedAt` is free on one branch and a single bounded read on the others — it is not free unconditionally**, and the difference is named because a plan writer told it is free hits a branch the spec says cannot exist. The tick has the document in hand only when the per-tick event budget trips *inside* a page. A loop that checks its budget **between** pages — the natural implementation, and the certain one if the budget is a multiple of the page size — has nothing in hand; so does a tick whose ingest phase aborted on a contained fault part-way through a page, which may have applied a prefix the cursor has not yet advanced over. On both of those branches the tick performs **one extra indexed `findOne`** over `ops_events` sorted `{ publishedAt: 1, _id: 1 }` for the first document strictly after the *cursor's current position*, which is correct on both (after a mid-page fault the cursor still names the last fully-applied event). It is a cheap, bounded read on the same index the ingest sort uses, and those branches are already paying for the saturating `countDocuments` beside it. The implementer may instead define the stop to always leave a page in hand and keep the free form; what is fixed here is that **`oldestUnappliedAt` is always reported**, by one of the two mechanisms, and never silently omitted on a boundary or fault stop. `oldestUnappliedAt` is the better of the two against the event TTL — it is an age, compared directly against `config.activity.retentionDays`, where a count is only a proxy — and it is the one a reader should surface for the gap. The count remains the load signal.

**Three of these fields are the stall gauges — depths, not fault counters, and never to be read as rates.** `rowsSubscriptionUnresolved`, `rowsTransportUnbound` and `rowsCadenceUnresolved` are the **standing depth of the stall set** — one per `stalledReason`, so each of the three ways a tick can decline a row is separately visible — derived each tick from three bounded saturating `countDocuments` over the sparse `{ stalledReason: 1, state: 1 }` index (⚠ limit delegated, the same shape as `eventsBehind`, and read as "at least N"). **The `state ∈ {pending, delivered}` clause is part of the index, not a filter over it**, and it is required for correctness rather than speed: an acknowledgement or a clearing fact moves a stalled row out of the working states without unsetting its stall marker, so a marker-only count would keep counting rows that are no longer stuck on anything. They are deliberately **not** derived from the delivery phase's own filter pass: a stalled row is only re-observed once per `STALL_RECHECK_MS`, so a pass-derived gauge would read near-zero between re-checks and spike on them, which is exactly the wrong signal. Because they are counts, a single mis-registered adapter or unregistered cadence re-counts on every tick for as long as it stands. That is correct for a gauge — "this many rows are currently stuck on X" is the operator's question — and would be badly wrong as an incident count. They are named separately from `subscriptionUnresolved` / `transportUnbound` / `cadenceUnresolved` in D8's counter list for exactly this reason: those are cumulative per-process totals, these are the current standing depth.

⚠ **Named residual, bounded to one tick:** neither the snooze-expiry arm nor the intake arms nor the clearing arm unsets a row's `stalledAt`/`stalledReason` — only an attempt does (step 5). A row that was stalled and then re-enters a working state (a snooze expiring, a `seen`/`dismissed` row renewed, a `cleared` row reopened) therefore carries a stale marker until its next attempt, and the three gauges over-count it for at most that one tick, because such a row arrives with `nextNudgeAt = now` and is attempted on the very next delivery phase. The alternative — clearing the markers at each of those arms — is four more write sites for a one-tick reading error on a gauge already read as "at least N", and the `state ∈ {pending, delivered}` clause in the index means a row that goes the other way (stalled, then acknowledged or cleared) drops out immediately rather than lingering.

**`rowsUnknownOutcome` is a fourth gauge and is indexed like the other three.** It is the standing depth of rows whose last attempt returned `unknown` — the uncertainty C8 requires every reader to surface — derived from a bounded saturating `countDocuments` over the sparse `{ lastOutcome: 1, state: 1 }` index (D2), read as "at least N". The index is required rather than nice: without one this is an unindexed collection scan on every 30 s tick over a ledger whose working-state rows never TTL (D9), and it is the **only** field on this heartbeat that would be unbounded — `rowsPending`, `rowsNudgeDue` and `rowsSnoozed` are each served by a prefix of an index the D2 table already carries, and `eventsBehind` and the three stall gauges were given bounded forms above for exactly this reason. It is deliberately **not** derived from the delivery phase's own outcomes, for the same reason as the three stall gauges but with a sharper edge: a pass-derived figure would be a *rate* (how many attempts this tick came back uncertain), while the operator's question is a depth (how many rows are sitting on an uncertain outcome right now), which persists across every tick in which those rows are not attempted at all — and under an unregistered cadence that is every tick.

### D6. The transport interface and the one adapter

The interface is KPR-458 D6 verbatim and is not amended:

```typescript
interface OpsTransport {
  readonly adapterId: string;
  validateTarget(target: unknown): boolean;
  deliver(n: NotificationView): Promise<DeliveryOutcome>;
}
```

`DeliveryOutcome`, `NonacceptanceReason` and `UncertaintyReason` are D6's closed sets, transcribed unchanged into `src/ops/transport.ts`. An adapter never extends them and maps everything it cannot prove to `unknown`.

**`NotificationView` is structured and carries the resolved target**, which is a faithful reading of D6 rather than an amendment: `deliver` takes exactly one argument, so the target the notifier resolved from `subscription.transport.target` must ride the view. D6 bars an adapter from *choosing or discovering* a recipient; being handed one is the whole point.

```typescript
interface NotificationView {
  handle: string;                                  // opaque; names one ledger row
  target: unknown;                                 // adapter-scoped, resolved by the notifier, opaque to it
  event: { producer; reasonId; class; retry; waiting; subject; generation;
           dedupeKey; publishedAt; detail; evidence };
  remediation?: { template: string; parameters: OpsDetail };
  ledger: { state; eventCount; nudgeCount; attemptCount; firstSeenAt; lastEventAt };
}
```

Never in the view: a rendered message string, a subscriber id, a channel or person name, a severity, an owner, or anything D2 lists as explicitly absent. The adapter owns rendering, because a Slack block, an SMS body and a push payload are different artifacts of the same fact.

**`remediation` is resolved from `ops_reasons`, read-only.** The notifier loads its own `(producer, reasonId) → OpsReason` map at `init()` through KPR-454's `OpsStore.loadReasons()` — the same no-reload posture the publisher takes, so **restart is the lever** and a template change lands at the next boot. An unknown reason omits `remediation` and never blocks delivery (counted `reasonUnknown`). A pure `renderRemediation(template, detail)` helper performs `{key}` substitution over allow-listed parameters only; adapters may call it, and it is offered rather than imposed.

⚠ **`loadReasons()` is a heavier borrow than "read-only reuse" implies, and the cost is accepted rather than unnoticed.** It returns `{ map: Map<string, LoadedReason>, anomalies }` where `LoadedReason` is `{ row, detailSchema }` (`kpr-454-plan-3-publisher.md:62-64, 197-205`): per row it runs `auditReasonRow` — warning once per anomaly — and compiles a zod object from `detailKeys`. The notifier needs neither, only `remediationTemplate`. So calling it duplicates the publisher's boot-time anomaly warnings and re-compiles every schema. **Accepted, measured:** `init()` runs exactly once per boot and reasons never reload, so the cost is one extra warn set and one extra compile pass per process — not per turn, not per tick — and the duplicated lines are identical text from a second module, which is noise rather than a false signal. The alternative that Integration point 1 already reserves — an additive `OpsStore` read returning rows uncompiled and unaudited — is the right move only if a later measurement says otherwise; it is a cross-child edit to a `ready-to-implement` sibling and is not worth spending here.

**`validateTarget` is called at subscription load, not at code registration — and it can only be called for an adapter that exists.** Subscriptions are operator-registered Mongo rows, so "registration time" for a target is the moment the notifier loads it. But adapters are registered **below** the spawn-capable boundary (D10), after `index.ts:639`, so a subscription load that ran in `init()` would have no adapter to validate against for **any** subscription. That combination is not merely undefined here, it is silently destructive: the cursor survives a restart while the map does not, so a warm restart whose first sweep pass covers the down-time backlog would resolve every stamped id against an empty map, create zero rows, and leave those events permanently under the cursor. The rule, fixed rather than left to two rules pointing opposite ways:

- **The first subscription load happens in `start()`, not `init()`** (D10), which is after `registerTransport(...)`. Nothing intake consumes comes from that map, so nothing is lost by deferring it, and it matches D12's own init/start split.
- A subscription whose `adapterId` names **no registered adapter loads normally, unvalidated**. It is not an invalid target; it is a target nobody can judge yet. Its rows are created and are left to delivery-time `transportUnbound` (D5 step 3) — the single, already-specified home for an unbound adapter.
- A subscription whose adapter **is** registered and whose target that adapter **rejects** is **unloaded in memory** — never mutated in the database, because operator data is not the engine's to rewrite — warned **once per process, memoized on `subscriptionId`**, and counted `subscriptionUnloaded`. Per *process*, for the same reason as D5's profile warning: the map reloads every 60 s, so "once per load" would be one line per rejected subscription per minute for as long as the misregistration stands. The standing depth is the `subscriptionUnloaded` counter and the `rowsTransportUnbound` gauge, which is what those are for.
- A `validateTarget` that **throws** rather than returning `false` is caught at the load site and treated exactly as `false` — the subscription is unloaded, warned and counted on the same branch. An adapter that cannot decide is not a reason to load a target nobody judged, and it must never abort the load of the remaining subscriptions.

The two edge-case rows stay distinct because they now describe distinct states: "target-invalid at ingest" is a judged rejection; "adapter not registered" is an unjudged binding.

**The subscription map** is first loaded in `start()` (above) and refreshed on a 60 s timer and on the existing `SIGUSR1` handler, mirroring KPR-454 D9 exactly (`unref()`ed, cleared in `stop()`, a fault leaves the previous set in place and counts, never empties it). D12 already rules that a reload lag means a just-registered subscription may miss an event by seconds, and D5 forbids retrospective enrolment either way.

⚠ **The reload is deliberately duplicated, not a missed DRY opportunity.** Post-KPR-454 the engine holds **two** 60 s timers over `ops_subscriptions` — the publisher's and this one — and **two** lines in the one `SIGUSR1` handler. That reads as an obvious consolidation target and it should not be consolidated on that ground alone: the two are different projections of the same rows for different purposes at different lifecycle points. The publisher needs compiled *filters* and loads in its own `init()`; the notifier needs `transport` bindings validated against registered adapters and therefore cannot load before `start()` (above). A shared loader would have to be either the union — dragging filter compilation above the boundary into a component that must never evaluate a match (D12, AC2) — or a cache with two shapes and two readiness points, which is more machinery than two `setInterval`s. The cost is one extra `find()` per minute over a collection sized by the operator's subscriber count. If a later child consolidates them, this paragraph is the argument it has to beat.

**The one adapter: Slack.** `SlackOpsTransport`, `adapterId: "slack"`, registered only when Slack is configured. It is a **separate class from `SlackObligationPoster`** and the two are never merged: KPR-456's poster returns KPR-456's own `PostOutcome` taxonomy under a contract canon explicitly preserves, and a DRY-motivated unification would make one contract's change a silent change to the other. What is copied is the hardening, item for item (`slack-post.ts:54-150`): the fixed `chat.postMessage` endpoint, `allowAbsoluteUrls: false`, `redirect: "error"`, the `x-slack-req-id` origin check, HTTP 200 **plus** `ok: true` plus a channel/ts shape check, `mrkdwn: false` / `parse: "none"` / `link_names: false` / no unfurl, and a bounded body.

Its vendor mapping is **exactly** KPR-456's proven `NONACCEPTANCE` list (`slack-post.ts:4-18`) projected onto D6's set, with nothing added:

| Slack code | `NonacceptanceReason` |
| --- | --- |
| `not_authed`, `invalid_auth`, `token_expired`, `token_revoked` | `unauthenticated` |
| `missing_scope`, `no_permission`, `ekm_access_denied`, `restricted_action` | `unauthorized` |
| `channel_not_found` | `target-unknown` |
| `not_in_channel`, `is_archived` | `target-ineligible` |
| `rate_limited`, `ratelimited`, or an origin-verified HTTP 429 | `refused-rate-limit` |

`payload-rejected` is in the type and has **no Slack mapping**, deliberately: D6 (carrying KPR-456's rule into both contracts) admits a new nonacceptance mapping only with documented evidence that the response proves nonacceptance, plus a classifier test. Everything else — timeout, malformed body, unrecognized code, crash after submission — is `unknown`.

**The echo registration is a correctness requirement, not hygiene.** Every accepted post calls `SlackGateway.registerOutboundTs(channel, ts)` (`slack-gateway.ts:608`), exactly as KPR-456 wires it at `index.ts:639`. Without it, an ops notification posted into a channel the bot listens to is re-ingested as a `WorkItem` and **spawns an agent turn** — which is D10 invariant (b) and C14 violated, and worse, a turn that fails a tool republishes and the loop closes. `deliveryReference` is `{ kind: "slackMessage", id: "<channel>:<ts>" }` (a reference, never a URL — C13).

### D7. Acknowledgement intake — the seam KPR-455 calls

Intake is D12 item (5) and belongs here, not on the adapter: the ledger is the only thing an acknowledgement writes, D6 bars an adapter from touching it, and D7's rules are the same ones this component already enforces on every other transition. It is **the one part of this component that is not swept** — a direct, bounded write on the acknowledging caller's own path, because an acknowledgement that takes a sweep interval to register invites the operator to press the button twice.

**The API KPR-455's edge calls**, and the whole of the seam:

```typescript
type OpsAck = "seen" | "dismissed" | "snoozed";

interface OpsAcknowledgement {
  handle: string;          // the handle from the NotificationView; opaque, never parsed by the caller
  act: OpsAck;
  actorId: string;         // a stable id — a Slack user id, an agent slug; never a display name
  at: Date;                // the instant of the vendor act, not the edge's receipt time (see idempotence)
  snoozedUntil?: Date;     // required for "snoozed"
}

type OpsIntakeRefusal =
  | "unknown-handle" | "unattributed" | "row-cleared"
  | "integrity-dismissal" | "snooze-not-future" | "illegal-transition";

// D7's closed six, and nothing else may be added to it here — it is contract, not this child's
type OpsNotificationState =
  | "pending" | "delivered" | "seen" | "dismissed" | "snoozed" | "cleared";

type OpsIntakeResult =
  | { state: "applied"; rowState: OpsNotificationState; snoozedUntil?: Date }  // clamped value echoed back
  | { state: "noop"; reason: "already-applied" | "superseded" }
  | { state: "refused"; reason: OpsIntakeRefusal }
  | { state: "unavailable" };                                                  // not started, stopping, or storage fault

accept(input: OpsAcknowledgement): Promise<OpsIntakeResult>   // never throws
```

**The handle** is the ledger row's `_id` rendered as a hex string. It is opaque by contract: no consumer parses it, and its derivation may change. It **names** a row and confers no authority — D9's model is attribution, not authorization, and every act records who performed it. ⚠ Named residual: a handle round-tripped through a vendor payload (a Slack action `value`) can in principle be altered by the actor, so a determined workspace member could acknowledge a row they were not shown. The act is still attributed to them, which is the contract's stated remedy; nothing here adds an authorization model, and doing so would be policy this component may not hold.

**One derived value governs every clock-bearing write: `anchorAt = min(at, now)`.** `at` is the *caller's* assertion of when the act happened, and D6 names an agent tool call and an operator CLI invocation among the inbound surfaces, so it is **not** always a signed vendor timestamp — a mis-written KPR-455 edge or an agent passing `at = now + 10y` must not be able to steer the ledger's clock. An `at` in the past is honest and is used as given (a vendor act genuinely precedes its callback); an `at` ahead of the server clock is not, and is pulled back. `anchorAt` is what step 7's snooze ceiling is measured from — which is what makes the clamp that "must always exist" unbypassable rather than caller-supplied — and it is also what step 8 writes to `stateAt` and `principalAt`, and therefore what D9's `expiresAt` derives from, so an edge cannot shorten or extend a per-person retention horizon at will. This satisfies KPR-458's "measured from the instant of the act" for any honest `at` and refuses to be steered by a dishonest one. The **one** value that keeps the raw `at` is `lastAckKey` (step 3): the idempotence key must be a pure function of the caller's input, or a retried duplicate whose `at` was clamped against a *later* `now` would derive a different key and re-apply.

**The check order is load-bearing and is fixed here**, because three of its steps are order-sensitive in ways a natural implementation gets wrong:

1. **Resolve the handle** to exactly one row. Malformed or unresolvable ⇒ `refused: "unknown-handle"`.
2. **Attribute or refuse.** A missing, blank or non-string `actorId` ⇒ `refused: "unattributed"`. **And `actorId === OPS_SYSTEM_PRINCIPAL` ⇒ `refused: "unattributed"` on the same branch** — an explicit refusal, not an assumption. Without it, a caller passing the reserved id (a mis-written KPR-455 edge, an operator command defaulting its actor, a transport handing back its own service identity) lands `seen`/`dismissed`/`snoozed` attributed to the system principal, which D7 rule 5 and C9 forbid absolutely — "the system principal can never produce those three states". AC8 asserts that property; this step is what establishes it, and D2's colon-bearing constant shape is what makes the comparison decidable rather than a prose judgment. Never attributed to the adapter, to a service account, or to the system principal.
3. **Idempotence replay check, before legality.** An incoming act whose `${actorId}:${act}:${at.toISOString()}` equals the row's `lastAckKey` ⇒ `{ state: "noop", reason: "already-applied" }`. This must precede step 6 because `dismissed` is **not** a "from" state in D7's table: a duplicated dismissal callback checked in the other order returns `refused: "illegal-transition"`, which is a lie about a no-op and exactly the kind of error an inbound edge would then try to handle.
   **And a monotonicity guard on the same step**, because `lastAckKey` alone has the exact weakness D3 correctly rejects for events — it records only the *last* applied act, so a **delayed duplicate of an older one** slips past it. A Slack retry of a `seen` arriving after the actor's later `snoozed` misses the key comparison, passes step 6 (`snoozed → seen` is legal), and **regresses the row**: it cancels a live snooze and rewinds `stateAt`/`expiresAt`. So the row also carries `lastAckAt`, the **`anchorAt`** of the last applied act, and an incoming act whose own `anchorAt` is **not strictly after** it ⇒ `{ state: "noop", reason: "superseded" }`. This is D3's apply-if-strictly-newer, symmetric at the row level. It is the anchored value on **both** sides deliberately: storing a raw future `at` here would lock the row out of every honest act that followed, converting a bad timestamp into permanent unacknowledgeability — the same class of defect the clamp anchor closes in step 7.
   ⚠ **The anchor is `lastAckAt`, deliberately not `principalAt`**, which is the obvious-looking field and is wrong: `principalAt` advances on *system* transitions too (a delivery, a snooze expiry, a clear), so a nudge landing one second after a human's click would make that human's acknowledgement look stale and no-op it — losing a real act and nudging forever, which is worse than the regression the guard exists to prevent. Named residual, accepted: two genuinely distinct acts bearing the **identical** `at` (a vendor payload with second granularity, say) resolve to a no-op on the second. That errs toward not-applied for an act the actor can repeat, which is the safe side of a race that also requires them to have acted twice inside one timestamp tick.
4. **`cleared` refusal.** Any act on a `cleared` row ⇒ `refused: "row-cleared"`. D7 admits no such transition, and this is the ordinary race in which a clearing fact lands between a delivery and the acknowledger's click. Nothing is recorded, and KPR-455's edge must treat this refusal as **benign** — a no-op for the acknowledger, not an error they must surface.
5. **`integrity` dismissal refusal.** `act === "dismissed"` on a row whose snapshotted `class` is `integrity` ⇒ `refused: "integrity-dismissal"` (D7 rule 3: if an integrity condition can be dismissed like an ordinary alert, someone eventually dismisses one and a gate is skipped).
6. **Transition legality** against D7's table: the from-state must be one of `pending`, `delivered`, `seen`, `snoozed`. Anything else ⇒ `refused: "illegal-transition"`.
7. **Snooze validation and the clamp.** `act === "snoozed"` with `snoozedUntil` absent or not strictly after **`anchorAt`** ⇒ `refused: "snooze-not-future"` (D12: "not a pause at all"). Otherwise the value is **clamped and applied, never refused** (D12: refusing would leave the row unpaused and nudging, the opposite of what the acknowledger asked for):

   ```
   anchorAt     = min(at, now)
   snoozedUntil = min(requested, anchorAt + min(HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING, policy.maxSnoozeMs ?? HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING))
   ```

   **The ceiling is anchored on `anchorAt`, not on `at`, and that is what makes it a ceiling.** Anchored on the caller's `at` with no bound against the server clock, the only validation in this step is that `snoozedUntil` beats `at` — so an `at` far in the future yields an arbitrarily distant `snoozedUntil`, on an `integrity` row included: precisely "a terminal state by another name", which D12 and C10 forbid, reachable through a surface D6 explicitly opens to an agent tool call. Pulling the anchor back to the server clock costs one `min` and satisfies the contract's "measured from the instant of the act" for every honest `at`. The refusal test moves to `anchorAt` for the same reason: with a bogus future `at`, a genuinely future `snoozedUntil` would otherwise be refused as "not a pause".

   `policy` here is **the copy the last tick retained**, never a fresh read — D5 fixes that, and it is what keeps this step inside the "one indexed read plus one CAS" bound stated below. `?? HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING` therefore covers two paths, not one: no `ops_policy` document registered, **and** no tick having completed yet (intake is live from `init()` while the sweep starts later, D10). Both resolve to the hard ceiling, which is the correct answer for both.

   **A clamp must always exist**, which is why a contract-side hard ceiling lives in code (⚠ 7 days, delegated). D12 makes intake the only place a `snoozedUntil` enters the system precisely so that an unclamped one cannot become "a terminal state by another name", and an unregistered `ops_policy` must not be the hole that reintroduces one. The operator's registered maximum can only tighten it. The clamped value is echoed in the result so the edge can tell the human what it actually did.

8. **Apply**, as one CAS conditioned on the row's current `state`, `lastAckKey` **and `lastAckAt`** — the monotonicity guard is only as strong as its precondition, and re-reading it inside the CAS is what keeps a concurrent second act from landing out of order: set `state`, `stateAt = anchorAt`, `principal = actorId`, `principalAt = anchorAt`, `lastAckKey` (from the raw `at`, step 3), `lastAckAt = anchorAt`, `snoozedUntil` (or unset it for `seen`/`dismissed`), `expiresAt` **by D9's state invariant — derived from `stateAt`, so from `anchorAt`; set for `seen` and `dismissed`, and unset for `snoozed`, including on the `seen → snoozed` arm where the row arrives carrying a live one** — and **unset `nextNudgeAt` on all three acts**: for `seen`/`dismissed` alongside `forceDeliver` (both stop nudging, so a re-delivery owed from an earlier reopen or expiry is no longer owed), and for `snoozed` **as well**, because the row is suppressed until `snoozedUntil` and the expiry phase re-sets `nextNudgeAt = now` on the way back into a working state (D5). The `snoozed` arm deliberately does **not** clear `forceDeliver` — the re-delivery is still owed after the pause — and unsetting `nextNudgeAt` is what keeps that retained flag out of arm 2's index range while the row is not working (D5's scoping of the never-unset rule). A lost CAS — a sweep tick moved the row between read and write — retries **once** and then returns `{ state: "unavailable" }`.

   ⚠ **The CAS on an absent `lastAckAt` is written `{ lastAckAt: { $exists: false } }`, never a literal `undefined`.** A row that has never been acknowledged carries no `lastAckAt`, so the first act's precondition is an absence. Passing `undefined` in the filter happens to work against this repo today only because `ignoreUndefined` is not set anywhere in `src/` (verified at this tree) and the driver therefore serializes `undefined → null`, which matches a missing field; that is an accident of an unset client option, not a guarantee, and a later `MongoClient` option would silently turn every first acknowledgement into a lost CAS. The same rule applies to `lastAckKey`.

**Re-snoozing an already-`snoozed` row moves `snoozedUntil` and nothing else** (D7's table) — beyond the attribution and idempotence marks every applied act writes (`stateAt`, `principal`, `principalAt`, `lastAckKey`, `lastAckAt`), which are not row *content* in D7's sense. **`snoozed` does not clear and `dismissed` does not clear** — only a class-legal clearing fact does (D4).

**Bounded and contained.** Intake is one indexed read plus one CAS under a wall-clock deadline (⚠ delegated), never throws, never publishes (D10 invariant (a)), never spawns a turn (invariant (b)), and records **the principal and the instant and nothing else** — no display name, no email, no device, no client, no location, and deliberately no free-text reason for a dismissal (D9).

**What the edge owes, restated so KPR-455 has it in one place** (D6, unchanged and binding on that child, not this one): convert one vendor-specific act into one `accept(...)` call and do nothing else; never read or write the ledger or `ops_subscriptions`; hold no state; attribute or refuse — a read receipt, a channel visit, an emoji reaction or elapsed time is **not an act**, and this edge must not manufacture one from any of them. **Pass `at` as the vendor act's own instant, at full precision — never the edge's receipt time, and never truncated.** That clause belongs in this list rather than only in the delegated bullet at the end of this spec, because *both* of intake's guarantees rest on it: the idempotence key is `${actorId}:${act}:${at.toISOString()}`, so a substituted receipt time makes every retry a distinct key and a genuine duplicate double-applies; and the monotonicity guard orders acts by `anchorAt`, so truncating a vendor timestamp's sub-second precision collapses two genuinely distinct acts into one and no-ops the second. An edge that has only a coarse timestamp should pass it as it is and accept the named residual, not synthesize a finer one. Idempotency, class legality and state legality are intake's job, so a duplicate callback is safe and the edge needs no memory.

### D8. Single-flight, containment, and the fault counters

**Single-flight per row** (D12). Ticks never overlap and a tick acts on a row at most once, so intra-sweep concurrency is impossible by construction. The one genuine race is intake against a tick on the same row, and it is **serialized, never merged**: a per-row in-process latch (a `Map<rowId, Promise>` chain, entries deleted on settle so the map is bounded by in-flight work) orders them, and every ledger write is additionally a CAS on the precondition it depends on, so a lost race is *detected* rather than blended. Cross-process concurrency is out of model — KPR-456 canon fixes the single-engine assumption and this child introduces no new work identity, no lease, and no elapsed-time reclamation.

**Containment, in D12's three separate senses, which must not be merged.**

(a) *Never fails, delays or alters a turn.* None of this component's work is on a turn path, and that is structural rather than promised: nothing in the sweep, the transports, or intake is imported by `agent-runner.ts`, `tool-bridge.ts`, `dispatcher.ts` or `agent-manager.ts`, and an import-graph test pins it (AC2). Intake runs on the acknowledging caller's own path — an operator command, a transport callback, or an agent's tool call on a turn it was already running — so its bounded write is the acknowledger's own cost.

(b) *A tick contains its own faults* — **and the ingest phase's containment is a different rule from every other phase's, because the cursor's no-skip guarantee rests on it.** "Per-row `try`/`catch`, counted, continue" is the delivery and snooze-expiry rule and is **wrong for ingest**; stating it as a blanket rule is what produces a silent, permanent event loss.

- **Ingest** (the event-driven phase: clearing application and row creation/renewal, D4). **The unit of containment is the event, not the row.** A fault anywhere in applying event `e` — its clearing fan-out, or any one of its per-subscription renewals — means `e` is not fully applied. The phase therefore **stops at `e`**: nothing later in the page is applied, the fault is counted, and the cursor advances **no further than `e`'s predecessor** — it may be written to that partial position or left where the tick found it, since either way the next tick re-reads a prefix of already-applied, CAS-guarded events — so in neither case is there **a hole behind it**. The page from `e` onward is re-read on the next tick, which is safe and cheap because every application is a CAS'd apply-if-newer write (D3) — the rows that did apply on the first pass are total no-ops on the retry. **Per-row continue is forbidden here**, and the reason is worth naming because the literal reading is the natural one: an implementation that caught the fault at event 5, applied 6…N and advanced the cursor to N would leave event 5 permanently unnotified, uncounted, and invisible to `eventsBehind` — not *behind* the cursor but *under* it, exactly the shape of the second-producer residual D3 names in full, except manufactured inside this component rather than inherited from another. D3's "the cursor is written to the last fully-applied event's position" is that rule; this is where it is defined.
- **Delivery and snooze expiry.** Per-row `try`/`catch`, counted, the phase continues to the next row. These phases have no cursor and no ordering obligation — each row's write is an independent CAS on that row alone — so a skipped row costs that row one tick and nothing else, and the stall and the arm split (D5) already bound what a repeatedly-faulting row can do to the phase.
- Per-phase `ok` flags feed the heartbeat's `degraded` state; ticks never overlap; a stuck adapter call is bounded by the adapter's own deadline, not by the sweep's patience.

(c) *Faults are counted, never published.* D10 invariant (a), and this is where it earns its keep: a wire that published its own delivery, nudge or intake failures into the surface it serves would be the feedback loop the invariant forbids. Counters live in memory behind a `getSnapshot()` and in the heartbeat document; **nothing about the ops path is ever published as an `ops_event`**, on any path, including the transports. And invariant (b) binds absolutely: **no delivery may synchronously spawn an agent turn** — the Slack echo registration in D6 is the concrete mechanism, not a nicety.

**Counters**, exposed by `getSnapshot()` and mirrored to the heartbeat: `eventsApplied`, `rowsCreated`, `rowsRenewed`, `renewalStale`, `rowsCleared`, `clearRefused`, `clearNoRow`, `deliveriesAccepted`, `deliveriesRejected`, `deliveriesUnknown`, `transportFaults`, `subscriptionUnresolved`, `transportUnbound`, `cadenceUnresolved`, `reasonUnknown`, `subscriptionUnloaded`, `intakeApplied`, `intakeNoop`, `intakeRefused`, `intakeUnavailable`, `cursorReinitialized`, `sweepFaults`, `subscriptionReloadFaults`, `policyReadFaults`, `indexFailures`.

### D9. Retention: the ledger TTL and the acknowledgement-silence horizon

D9 requires a row to be **retained while it is still working** — `pending`, `delivered`, `snoozed` — and to TTL from `stateAt` once it has stopped — `seen`, `dismissed`, `cleared` — so attributed actor data ages out with the row that carries it. A Mongo TTL index cannot be state-conditional, so the conditional lives in a field. **The rule is an invariant on the resulting state, not an enumeration of transitions** — every write that changes `state` also writes `expiresAt`, to `stateAt + ledgerRetention` when the new state is one of the three stopped ones (`seen`, `dismissed`, `cleared`) and to unset otherwise (`pending`, `delivered`, `snoozed`). On the intake path `stateAt` is the clock-anchored `anchorAt` rather than the caller's raw `at` (D7), so this horizon is not caller-steerable in either direction. Stated as an invariant deliberately: an enumeration is what gets an out-transition missed, and one **was** missed in an earlier draft of this spec — **`seen → snoozed`**, which D7's table admits (`kpr-458-design.md:303`) and this spec's own step-6 legality check explicitly permits (`from ∈ {pending, delivered, seen, snoozed}`). A `seen` row carries a live `expiresAt`; a subscriber who then snoozes it moves it into a *working* state, and under an enumeration that omitted this arm the row could be TTL-deleted **while snoozed**, contradicting contract D9's "retained while it is still working — `pending`, `delivered`, `snoozed`" (`kpr-458-design.md:344`). The out-transitions are therefore `seen → snoozed`, the `cleared → pending` reopen, and the snooze expiry (`snoozed → delivered | pending`, which starts unset and stays unset) — but the invariant, not that list, is what an implementation checks. The index is `{ expiresAt: 1 }, expireAfterSeconds: 0`. A TTL on `stateAt` instead would delete working rows and is the specific mistake this paragraph exists to prevent.

**The value is `min(30 days, config.activity.retentionDays)`.** ⚠ Delegated, non-blocking, and composed rather than copied for a stated reason: D9 rules that "an operator who wants longer retention of ops history should extend the event TTL and not the ledger's, so the behavioural half ages first", and KPR-454 binds the event TTL to `config.activity.retentionDays` (default 90, `src/config.ts:659`). A fixed 30 days satisfies the direction invariant at the default and violates it for an operator who sets activity retention to 14; the `min` keeps the invariant true for *any* setting with no new `hive.yaml` key. **No lever ships** (the standing "no preemptive levers" preference); raising the horizon is a code change.

D9's named consequence is carried here unchanged and must be stated to an operator rather than discovered: **acknowledgement silence lasts until the condition recovers and recurs (a new epoch, KPR-454 D8) or until this TTL elapses, whichever comes first.** Once a row ages out, a still-recurring condition re-mints under the same `dedupeKey` at `pending` and nudges again as though never acknowledged. Deliberate in both directions — a per-person behavioural record should not be kept indefinitely for a condition nobody ever cleared, and, as everywhere in this design, the race errs toward a duplicate nudge rather than a silent gap.

`ops_policy` carries no TTL: it is operator configuration, not history.

### D10. Boot order, start, and shutdown drain

**Above the boundary** (`src/index.ts:474`), in the wiring block ending at `:466`: construction, `await opsNotifier.init()` (collections, indexes, the reason map), and `setOpsNotifier(opsNotifier)` — a module-global singleton with a test reset, the `provider-registry.ts` / KPR-454 `publisher-singleton.ts` precedent. Unset ⇒ every intake call returns `{ state: "unavailable" }`, which keeps the pre-wiring boot window and every bare test construction correct by construction.

**`init()` deliberately does *not* load subscriptions, and does not arm the reload timer.** Both move to `start()`. The reason is D6's: adapters are registered below the boundary, so a load running in `init()` could validate no target against any adapter, and the "unloaded on validation failure" rule would then have to mean either "unload everything" or "validate nothing" — the first silently destructive across a warm restart, the second leaving `validateTarget` dead code. Nothing above the boundary needs the map: intake resolves a handle to a row and CASes it, consulting no subscription. This is D12's own init/start split, drawn where the dependency actually falls.

**Intake is available from the moment `init()` returns and `setOpsNotifier` runs — not from `start()`.** Deliberate: intake depends on nothing `start()` provides, and gating it on `start()` would widen its dead window across the whole Slack connect for no gain. A restart's ledger rows survive in Mongo, so an acknowledgement arriving in that window is a legitimate write against a real row, and the per-row latch (D8) is a no-op when no tick is running. `{ state: "unavailable" }` therefore means exactly three things: the singleton is unset (pre-wiring, or a bare test construction), `init()` did not complete, or `stop()` has begun.

The ticket fixes this placement, and the reason it is right rather than merely mandated: **intake is reachable from a turn.** D6 names an agent tool call as one of the inbound surfaces, so once KPR-455 wires an agent-facing edge the intake singleton *is* a per-spawn read; wiring it above the boundary now is what keeps that true without a later move — which is exactly the class of silent breakage KPR-414 was spent removing.

**A `SIGUSR1` arriving before `start()` is harmless.** The handler is registered at `src/index.ts:575` — below the boundary but above `opsNotifier.start()` — so a reload signalled in that window runs the notifier's reload line against a map that has not been loaded yet. Nothing breaks: the reload loads the subscriptions **unvalidated** (no adapter is registered yet, which is the already-specified "unjudged binding" branch of D6), no sweep is running to consume the result, and `start()`'s own first load runs afterwards with the adapters bound and re-validates every target. The window is one boot's worth of Slack connect and the worst case is one wasted `find()`.

⚠ **Naming collision, the same trap KPR-454's plan flags:** `src/ops/notifier-singleton.ts` exports a reader named `opsNotifier()`. `index.ts` imports only `setOpsNotifier`, so the local `const opsNotifier` is unambiguous — but the boot-order anchors are the literal strings `await opsNotifier.init()` and `setOpsNotifier(`, so the local variable name is load-bearing for the guard and the reader import must never be added to `index.ts` without renaming one of the two.

**`init()` is non-fatal to boot, with a split posture** mirroring KPR-454 D10 but drawn one line differently, because this child's unique index carries a correctness role (D2):

- The `(subscriptionId, dedupeKey)` **unique index failing leaves the notifier unstarted** — construction succeeds, the singleton is not set, the sweep never starts, and boot continues. Running the ledger without its identity guarantee is worse than not running it.
- Every other index fault is individually contained and counted and keeps the notifier usable.
- A reason-map load fault (in `init()`) or a first-subscription-load fault (in `start()`) leaves the notifier **unstarted**, for KPR-454's reason: a started notifier over an empty subscription map would create no rows and look identical to "nobody subscribed", spending an honest signal on a Mongo outage. The load moved, the posture did not — `start()` throwing its first load is contained by the same non-fatal-to-boot rule below, and leaves intake available and the sweep off.
- **"Unstarted" resolves differently for the two `init()`-side faults, and both answers are stated rather than left to the word.** A **reason-map** fault still runs `setOpsNotifier(opsNotifier)`: reasons are consumed only to attach `remediation` at delivery (D6), intake consumes none of them, and an acknowledgement arriving against an existing ledger row is a legitimate write whether or not this process could read `ops_reasons`. So intake is **live** and the sweep is off — the same posture as a `start()`-side load fault, reached one step earlier. A **unique-index** fault is the one case that does *not* set the singleton (above): without the ledger's identity guarantee, an intake CAS could resolve a handle against one of two rows for the same condition, so refusing the whole surface is correct there and only there. AC14 pins both.
- **Nothing here is fatal to boot.** An ops-diagnostics surface that can prevent the engine from starting is strictly worse than one that is absent, and "unstarted ⇒ inert" is this spec's defined safe state everywhere else.

**Below the boundary**, immediately after `await obligations.start(...)` at `index.ts:639` — that is, after `dispatcher.registerAdapter(slackAdapter)` (`:632`) and `await slackAdapter.start(...)` (`:634`):

```
opsNotifier.registerTransport(
  new SlackOpsTransport(config.slack.botToken, (c, ts) => slack.registerOutboundTs(c, ts)),
);
await opsNotifier.start();
```

The constructor takes **exactly those two arguments** — the bot token and the echo-registration callback — and the snippet is complete rather than elided, because the elision an earlier draft carried (`, ...`) is the kind a plan writer copies verbatim into a task. Everything else the transport needs (the fixed endpoint, the nonacceptance mapping, the hardening constants) is code-resident, and it is handed no target: the target rides each `NotificationView` (D6).

`start()` does the first subscription load (D6), arms the 60 s reload timer, and begins the sweep. This is the `workerPool.start()` disjoint-range precedent (`index.ts:815-828`) stated for a second subsystem: wiring-above-the-boundary and start-after-adapters have deliberately disjoint valid ranges, because a sweep that begins before its adapters exist would burn attempts against nothing (D12) — here, every due row would resolve `transportUnbound` and the counter would fill with boot noise. **The load's placement inside `start()` is load-bearing for the same reason and in the same direction**: it is what puts every subscription's `validateTarget` after every `registerTransport`, so the check has adapters to run against.

**Shutdown** adds `await opsNotifier.stop()` immediately after `await obligations.stop()` (`index.ts:943`), which places it before `slackAdapter.stop()` (`:975`) and `mongoClient.close()` (`:976`) — the KPR-456 ordering D12 names. `stop()` sets the stopped latch, clears the reload timer, stops accepting new intake, and awaits the in-flight tick, which exits at its next inter-row checkpoint; the drain is therefore bounded by one adapter deadline rather than being open-ended.

**`src/boot-order.test.ts` gains anchors in all three lists** — presence (a), the `wiringOffsets` array (b), and the `wiringStart` `Math.max` set (c), whose superset sweep must be bounded by the **latest** wiring anchor or a surface introduced between two wiring calls passes green. Adding to (a) alone is the exact failure mode the guard exists to catch. The two anchors are `await opsNotifier.init()` and `setOpsNotifier(`. `await opsNotifier.start()` sits **after** `wiringStart`, so (c)'s sweep does not see it and its `allowlist` needs no entry — a placement a later refactor must preserve or explicitly allowlist under the reviewed-classification discipline that list's comment demands. A second `describe` block, on the KPR-456 lifecycle-block pattern (`boot-order.test.ts:134-164`), pins the start-after-adapters and drain-before-Slack/Mongo orderings, and a negative-verify moves the wiring below the marker and confirms (b) and (c) both fail.

### D11. Levers: what an operator can turn off, and what rollback is

**No `hive.yaml` key ships.** Every lever is data, the KPR-454 posture and the standing "no preemptive levers" preference:

1. **Per-subscription kill switch.** `ops_subscriptions.enabled: false` on a row ⇒ within one reload (≤ 60 s) that subscription is unloaded, no new rows are created for it, and **its existing rows are neither delivered nor nudged**. Rows are left in place, never deleted, so re-enabling resumes exactly where it stopped. **The two directions have different latencies and both are stated:** stopping is ≤ one reload; resuming is ≤ one reload **plus one stall re-check interval** (D5), because a row the tick declined for an unresolved subscription is pushed forward rather than left at the head of the due-scan. This is the lever an operator reaches for at 2 a.m. — the direction that matters there is the fast one — and it is per-subscriber rather than global on purpose. **Re-enabling deliberately does *not* get an explicit un-stall**, and that is a settled call rather than a judgment one: clearing the stall markers on re-enable would mean an `updateMany` over `ops_notifications` scoped to that `subscriptionId`, which AC8 forbids by test ("no code path issues an `updateMany` against `ops_notifications`"), and per-row CAS'd un-stalling of an arbitrarily large set inside a reload is exactly the unbounded work the stall exists to prevent. The re-check interval is the price of the assertion, and it is paid on the slow direction only.
2. **The producer's own switch** is unchanged and upstream: `ops_reasons.enabled: false` + restart stops publication (KPR-454 D5).
3. **`ops_policy` absent or a cadence entry removed** ⇒ affected rows are attempted once per occurrence (delivered if the transport accepts) and never nudged between occurrences, and resume **within one stall re-check interval** after the entry is registered or restored, with no restart. That reversibility is a property of D5's realization, not a free one: the cadence-unresolved path **never unsets `nextNudgeAt`** — it pushes it forward — precisely so the rows stay visible to the due-scan and can be re-observed. Unsetting it would make this lever one-way (type bracketing, D5). "Within one re-check interval" rather than "on the first tick" is the deliberate cost of bounding the stall set's scan cost; it is a lever latency, not a delivery latency, and no *first* delivery is deferred by it that would otherwise have happened. (The qualifier is exact: the first nudge after a cadence is newly registered is deferred by up to one re-check interval — that delivery would not have happened at all without the registration, so nothing is lost, but the unqualified claim is literally false for it.)

**Rollback is a code revert.** No configuration flag turns the notifier off wholesale, because with zero subscription rows it is already inert and the per-subscription lever covers the live case.

**Two properties make merging ahead of KPR-455 honest, and one residual remains.** The sequencing constraint stands — D12 and this ticket's body both state that KPR-468 must not ship without KPR-455's inbound edge. What softens it to a *deployment* constraint rather than a code hazard is that (i) with zero `ops_subscriptions` rows no ledger row is ever created, and (ii) with no `ops_policy` cadence a row is **attempted once per occurrence (delivered if the transport accepts) and never nudged between occurrences** — one attempt on its first appearance, one on each recurrence after a clear, one on each snooze expiry, and nothing else. That is the precise form of the claim (D5): the looser "delivered once, ever" would be a *stronger* safety argument and is not what the code does, and the tighter "delivered once and never re-delivered" would strand a recurrence and break C10. **The residual:** an operator who registers both a subscription and a cadence before KPR-455 lands gets exactly the state D12 warns about — `judgment` and `integrity` rows, and `resource` conditions that never recover, nudging with nothing able to acknowledge them, and the only stop being lever 1 above. Recovering `resource` conditions still fall silent on their own, because D4's enable gate guarantees every `resource` reason has a registered clearing reason and a clearing fact reaches `cleared` with no acknowledgement.

### D12. Boundaries — what this child deliberately does not touch

- **KPR-454 is read-only and additive from here.** The notifier consumes `ops_events`, `ops_subscriptions` and `ops_reasons` through `OpsStore`'s existing handles and `loadReasons()` / `loadSubscriptions()`; it creates none of them, indexes none of them, writes none of them, and **never imports `src/ops/match.ts`**. Should the plan writer find a needed read that `OpsStore` does not expose, the change is **additive** — a new read method — never a modification of an existing one.
- **KPR-456 and KPR-457 keep their contracts.** Neither becomes an adapter of `OpsTransport`; `SlackObligationPoster` is not reused, re-exported, or refactored, and KPR-457's watchdog must not acquire a dependency on the thing it watches. KPR-456's unknown/no-automatic-resend rule and KPR-457's bounded-retry alert contract are separate and are not superseded by D6's taxonomy (canon, D10).
- **`agent_events` is unchanged**, including `EVENT_SCHEMAS`, `Scheduler.checkEvents` and the `subscribe: string[]` agent-definition field, which stays the event-bus domain list and is never overloaded to mean an ops subscription.
- **No MCP server is added**, so both KPR-390 standing obligations are inapplicable and are named here so a reader does not go looking: there is no `buildToolTransportInventory` descriptor to push and no `suppressAutoInjectedServers` gate to extend. `WORKER_SERVER_DENYLIST` and the `delegateServers` in-process constraint are untouched. An agent-facing acknowledgement tool would be an MCP surface subject to all of that — it is **KPR-455's** to decide and build, not this child's.
- **No reader, view, CLI, `hive doctor` section or dashboard.** The heartbeat document is a *write*; everything that reads it is KPR-455, and Gate 1 forbids the dashboard.
- **No catch-all.** No default subscription, fallback recipient, or fallback adapter is created by any code path (C3). An event that matched nobody produces no row, and that is the measurement.

## Edge cases

| Case | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Stamped subscription absent, disabled, or **target rejected by its registered adapter** at ingest | No row created (the subscription is not in the loaded map). The event's stored `matchedSubscriptions` still stands (D12) — it was honest for the set that existed at accept, and never a delivery promise. |
| Subscription whose `adapterId` names **no registered adapter** | **Loads normally, target unvalidated** — an unjudged binding is not an invalid one (D6). Rows are created and left to delivery-time `transportUnbound`; this is the distinct case from the row above. |
| Subscription disabled *after* rows exist | Rows are retained, not delivered, not nudged; re-enabling resumes. Latency ≤ one 60 s reload. |
| Clearing event for a `dedupeKey` with no row | Counted no-op. Openness is answered from the log, not the ledger (D8). |
| Clearing event failing its provenance test | Stored (by the publisher), row untouched, `clearRefused` counted. |
| Clearing event on an `informational` row | Never clears (D3). |
| Clearing event on a `dismissed` row | **Clears.** A dismissal stops nudging; only a clearing fact closes the condition. |
| Clearing or renewal replayed after a crash | No-op via the apply-if-newer watermark; `eventCount` does not double-count (C17). |
| Renewal on a `cleared` row | Reopens to `pending`, system principal, `stateEventId` = the renewing event, `nextNudgeAt = now`, **`forceDeliver: true`**, `expiresAt` unset (D7 rule 4). |
| Reopened row under **no** registered cadence | **Delivered**, via the `forceDeliver` disjunct. `attemptCount` is monotonic and a reopen does not reset it, so a two-disjunct gate would never deliver a condition that demonstrably came back (D5). |
| Expired snooze under **no** registered cadence | **Delivered**, via the same disjunct. A snoozed row's `attemptCount` is by construction > 0, so without it C10's "`snoozed` … expires" would resolve to permanent silence. |
| `forceDeliver` row whose subscription is disabled or whose adapter is unbound | Stalled, **flag retained** — only an attempt (or an intake `seen`/`dismissed`) clears it, so the delivery is still owed when the subscription or adapter comes back. |
| Renewal on `pending`/`delivered` | Counters advance; **`nextNudgeAt` is not reset** — a flapping producer must not drive the cadence. |
| New `generation` for the same subject/reason | A different `dedupeKey` ⇒ a fresh `pending` row beside the closed one. The closed one is never touched. |
| Row TTL'd out while the condition still recurs | Re-mints at `pending` and nudges again as though never acknowledged — D9's stated, deliberate consequence. |
| Cursor absent | Initialize to the server clock, warn, count `cursorReinitialized`. **No backfill.** |
| Cursor ahead of the newest event | Nothing to ingest; not an error. |
| Sweep so far behind that events hit the event TTL | The cursor advances past a gap; the heartbeat is the only signal — `oldestUnappliedAt` against the event TTL is the measure a reader must surface, with the bounded `eventsBehind` beside it as load. |
| Second concurrent producer into `ops_events` | **Out of model, and a precondition on the child that adds one** (D3). Its event can land under an advanced cursor and never be notified, invisible to every counter here. Not solved in this child; nothing today produces it. |
| Adapter `adapterId` not registered | Row untouched, `transportUnbound` counted. |
| Adapter throws instead of returning an outcome | Recorded as `unknown` / `transport-fault`, counted, contained. |
| `rejected` outcome | Recorded; no retry ladder; next attempt is the next ordinary nudge (D5, and D6 supplies no retry hint to schedule from). |
| `unknown` outcome | State unchanged — never reaches `delivered`; surfaced as uncertainty by every reader (C8). |
| No cadence resolvable for a row | **Attempted** once per occurrence (the `attemptCount === 0` and `forceDeliver` disjuncts) — delivered if the transport accepts — then **stalled**: `stalledReason: "cadence"`, `nextNudgeAt = now + STALL_RECHECK_MS ± jitter`. **Never unset** — an unset field is invisible to the `$lte` due-scan under type bracketing and would strand the row permanently — and never left at its due value either, which would park it permanently at the head of an ever-growing due-scan prefix (rows in `pending`/`delivered` never TTL, D9). Resumes within one re-check interval of a cadence being registered. |
| `rejected` or `unknown` outcome on a row with no resolvable cadence | Recorded; **no re-attempt** until the row's next occurrence, because the attempt gate is `attemptCount === 0 ∨ forceDeliver ∨ a cadence resolved` and none holds. Not a hot loop, not a stranding, and not a per-tick scan cost either — the row is stalled forward and re-checked on a bounded interval. |
| Stall set larger than the delivery phase's wall-clock budget | The guaranteed first delivery is **still made**: arm 1 (`attemptCount: 0`) and arm 2 (`forceDeliver`) are drawn before arm 3 and, more importantly, their indexes do not contain a stalled row at all — the immunity is structural, not merely an ordering. Arm 3's remainder defers to the next tick and is visible as backlog. |
| Subscription names an unknown cadence profile | **No fallback to the table** (D5's anti-merge rule); the row does not nudge, counted. |
| Snooze expiry | `delivered` if the row has a recorded `accepted` outcome, else `pending`; `nextNudgeAt = now`, `forceDeliver: true` (D7). The expiry phase runs **before** the delivery phase in the same tick, so an expiring row is re-delivered on that tick rather than waiting for the next. |
| Adapter's `validateTarget` **throws** at subscription load | Treated exactly as `false`: the subscription is unloaded in memory, warned once per process, counted `subscriptionUnloaded`. The remaining subscriptions still load. |
| Intake: unknown/malformed handle | `refused: "unknown-handle"`. |
| Intake: missing actor id | `refused: "unattributed"` — never attributed to a service principal. |
| Intake: `actorId` **equal to** `OPS_SYSTEM_PRINCIPAL` | `refused: "unattributed"`, on the same step-2 branch. The system principal can never produce `seen`/`dismissed`/`snoozed` (D7 rule 5, C9), and this is the check that makes that true rather than assumed. |
| Intake: `seen` row acknowledged as `snoozed` | Applied. `expiresAt` is **unset** on the way in — the row re-enters a working state and must not carry a live TTL (D9's state invariant). |
| Intake: `snoozed` applied to a row carrying `forceDeliver: true` | Applied. `nextNudgeAt` is **unset** (D7 step 8) and `forceDeliver` is **retained** — the re-delivery is still owed, and the expiry phase re-sets `nextNudgeAt = now`. Unsetting `nextNudgeAt` is what keeps the retained flag outside arm 2's index range while the row is not working, which is why arm 2's index carries no `state` key (D5). Reachable whenever an acknowledgement lands between the tick that set the flag and the tick that would have delivered it. |
| Intake before the first sweep tick has run | Available (intake is live from `init()`, D10); the snooze clamp falls back to `HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING`, the same answer as an unregistered `ops_policy`. |
| Intake: duplicate callback, identical `(actorId, act, at)` | `{ state: "noop", reason: "already-applied" }`, **checked before legality**, so a duplicated dismissal is not reported as an illegal transition. |
| Intake: delayed duplicate of an **older** act (a `seen` retry arriving after the actor's later `snoozed`) | `{ state: "noop", reason: "superseded" }` — the monotonicity guard on `lastAckAt`. Without it the act passes the `lastAckKey` check (which records only the *last* act), passes legality (`snoozed → seen` is legal), and cancels a live snooze. |
| Intake: `at` far ahead of the server clock | Anchored: `anchorAt = min(at, now)` governs the snooze ceiling, `stateAt`, `principalAt`, `expiresAt` and `lastAckAt`. A caller cannot buy an unbounded snooze, stretch or shorten a retention horizon, or lock a row out of future acknowledgement with a bad timestamp. `lastAckKey` alone keeps the raw `at`, so a retried duplicate still derives the same key. |
| Intake: any act on a `cleared` row | `refused: "row-cleared"` — benign for the acknowledger, not an error to surface. |
| Intake: `dismissed` on `integrity` | `refused: "integrity-dismissal"`. |
| Intake: `snoozed` with absent or non-future `snoozedUntil` | `refused: "snooze-not-future"`. |
| Intake: `snoozedUntil` beyond the maximum | **Applied clamped**, never refused; the clamped value is echoed back (D12). |
| Intake: no `ops_policy` registered | The hard ceiling still clamps — a clamp always exists. |
| Intake racing a sweep tick on the same row | Serialized by the per-row latch; a lost CAS retries once, then `{ state: "unavailable" }`. |
| Intake before `init()` completes, with the singleton unset, or during `stop()` | `{ state: "unavailable" }`. Never throws. **Not** gated on `start()` — intake consumes nothing `start()` provides (D10). |
| `ops_policy` read throws on a tick | The retained copy from an earlier tick is used; no stall marker is written and no `nextNudgeAt` is pushed forward. With no retained copy (a process's first tick) the delivery phase is skipped for that tick, counted `policyReadFaults`, heartbeat `degraded`. A throw is never resolved as "no policy registered" (D5). |
| Mongo unavailable for a tick | Tick fails, counted, heartbeat `state: "degraded"`, no throw, next tick retries. |
| Unique-index creation fails at `init()` | Notifier left **unstarted**; boot continues. |
| `waiting: human-now` event matching a subscription | Delivered normally. No engine default subscription selects `human-now`, and this child registers none (D10). |

## Acceptance criteria

Each maps to a KPR-458 criterion and is testable at this child's boundary.

1. **AC1 (C2, C3)** — With `ops_subscriptions` empty, ingesting any number of events creates **zero** `ops_notifications` documents and attempts zero deliveries; the events are unmodified. No code path creates a default subscription, fallback recipient or fallback adapter.
2. **AC2 (C2, C17, containment (a))** — Structural: the notifier never imports `src/ops/match.ts`, and no module under the notifier's own file set is imported by `agent-runner.ts`, `tool-bridge.ts`, `dispatcher.ts` or `agent-manager.ts`. A test asserts both over the source tree, so "off the turn path" is a checked property rather than a claim.
3. **AC3 (C17, the handoff)** — The sweep drives row creation **only** from each event's stamped `matchedSubscriptionIds`. Negative test: an event stamped with `[s1]` while an enabled subscription `s2` would also match its attributes creates a row for `s1` only. A second: a stamped id whose subscription has since been disabled creates no row and leaves the event's `matchedSubscriptions` count untouched.
4. **AC4 (C17, idempotence)** — Re-processing an arbitrary prefix of the log is a total no-op: `eventCount`, `state`, `attemptCount` and `nudgeCount` are byte-identical after a replay. **The able-to-fail case is the one D12's own phrasing misses:** a page containing two events `[e1, e2]` for one `dedupeKey`, replayed after a simulated crash between the last write and the cursor advance, must leave `eventCount` at **2**. **Both failing variants are predicted, because a wrong predicted number is what gets a genuine harness bug filed as an implementation bug.** With `$set: { latestEventId: e._id, … }` unconditional — the obvious reading of D12's rule — the replay admits e1 (→ 3, and `latestEventId` *regresses* to e1) and then admits e2 as well (→ **4**). A half-strengthened variant that refuses to regress `latestEventId` leaves it at e2 after e1 replays, so e2's own equality test then blocks it (→ **3**). The assertion `eventCount === 2` fails both, which is what makes the test sound against either; the numbers are recorded so a 3 and a 4 are read as the two known-wrong implementations rather than as noise. A third case: an out-of-order (older) event is discarded, not applied.
5. **AC5 (C17, the cursor)** — The cursor advances only after the page's ledger writes are acknowledged; a crash between them re-processes rather than skips. An **absent** cursor initializes to the clock, counts `cursorReinitialized`, and creates **no** rows for events already in the log — the negative test is a log seeded with 50 pre-existing events and an empty cursor, after which the ledger is empty.
6. **AC6 (clearing)** — A clearing event with **zero** `matchedSubscriptionIds` clears every row on the named `dedupeKey`; a match-scoped implementation fails this. Provenance: a `resource` row clears on a same-producer clearing event; a `judgment` and an `integrity` row do **not** clear on a clearing event with empty `evidence` and **do** clear with a reference; an `informational` row never clears; a `dismissed` row clears; a `cleared` row is a no-op. **The same-producer clause gets its own row rather than riding along incidentally:** a clearing event from producer `B` whose `clears` names a dedupeKey under producer `A` — with a reasonId that *is* in `B`'s registered `clearsReasonIds`, so it passes KPR-454's publish check, which constrains only the reasonId component — must **not** clear `A`'s row, and must increment `clearRefused`. Every failing test increments `clearRefused` and transitions nothing.
7. **AC7 (C8, C10, delivery and nudging)** — `accepted` transitions `pending → delivered`, records `deliveryReference`, and sets `nextNudgeAt` from the resolved cadence. `rejected` and `unknown` leave the state unchanged, and `unknown` never reaches `delivered`. **No automatic resend follows an `unknown`**; the row's next attempt is its next cadence-derived nudge, and a test drives the clock to prove the interval was the cadence's rather than a retry's. A nudge re-delivers the **same** row — a test asserts the `ops_notifications` document count is unchanged across ten nudges — and `nudgeCount` excludes the first attempt. There is no attempt ceiling and no terminal give-up. `attempts[]` never exceeds the ring cap while `attemptCount` keeps counting. **Scan-arm starvation is its own able-to-fail case**, because the property is structural and a single-query implementation passes every other assertion here: seed the ledger with more cadence-unresolved stalled rows than the delivery phase's budget can touch, force them all due (advance the clock past their re-check), then mint one fresh row and assert it is delivered **on that tick** — false of any implementation that serves one `{ state, nextNudgeAt }` scan in ascending order, since the stalled rows carry older `nextNudgeAt` and sort ahead of it.
8. **AC8 (C9, intake)** — Every transition to `seen`/`dismissed`/`snoozed` arrives through `accept(...)`, records a stable actor id and instant, and is idempotent: replaying one act returns `noop` and leaves the row byte-identical. **The order test is explicit:** a duplicated `dismissed` returns `{ state: "noop", reason: "already-applied" }` and **not** `refused: "illegal-transition"`. **The monotonicity guard gets its own able-to-fail case, because `lastAckKey` alone passes the duplicate test above and still fails this one:** apply `seen` at `t1`, then `snoozed` at `t2 > t1`, then replay the *`seen`* — it must return `{ state: "noop", reason: "superseded" }` and leave the row `snoozed` with its `snoozedUntil` and `expiresAt` byte-identical; a `lastAckKey`-only implementation applies it and cancels the snooze. A paired negative pins the anchor choice: a system transition (a nudge, a snooze expiry) between a human's act and its callback must **not** supersede that callback — which is false of any implementation that guards on `principalAt` instead of `lastAckAt`. **Clock anchoring is asserted directly:** an act with `at = now + 10 years` and `snoozedUntil = at + 1 day` is applied with `snoozedUntil ≤ now + HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING` and `stateAt ≈ now`, on an `integrity` row — the case that makes the clamp unbypassable rather than caller-supplied — and a following honest act by the same actor is **not** superseded. An act without an actor id is refused, never attributed to the system principal. **The system principal can never produce those three states, and the test drives the case that establishes it rather than the case that assumes it:** an `accept(...)` whose `actorId` *is* `OPS_SYSTEM_PRINCIPAL` returns `refused: "unattributed"` and leaves the row byte-identical. A second, structural: the constant matches neither the agent-id shape (lowercase letters, digits, hyphens) nor the Slack user-id shape, so no real actor can collide with it. `dismissed` on `integrity` is refused. Any act on a `cleared` row is refused. No bulk transition exists — a test asserts no code path issues an `updateMany` against `ops_notifications` — and no sweep path can set `dismissed`.
9. **AC9 (C14, D10 invariants)** — No code path in this diff publishes an `ops_event`, on success or on any fault; a fault injected into every phase produces log lines, counter increments and heartbeat `degraded`, and zero writes to `ops_events`. **The Slack transport registers every accepted post's `ts` through the echo callback** — a test asserts `registerOutboundTs` was called with the returned channel and ts, because without it the post re-enters as a `WorkItem` and spawns a turn. No delivery synchronously spawns an agent turn.
10. **AC10 (C10, the clamp)** — A snooze beyond the operator maximum is **applied clamped** and the clamped value is echoed; a snooze with no `ops_policy` registered is clamped to the hard ceiling; a snooze arriving **before the first tick has run** is likewise clamped to the hard ceiling (the cold path, D5/D7 step 7) rather than reading `ops_policy` itself — a test asserts intake performs no `ops_policy` read; a snooze with an absent or non-future `snoozedUntil` is refused. **The ceiling is anchored on the server clock, and the able-to-fail case is a caller-supplied `at`:** a snooze whose `at` is far in the future is applied with `snoozedUntil` no later than `now + HARD_SNOOZE_CEILING` — false of any implementation anchoring on `at`, which admits an arbitrarily distant pause, i.e. the terminal state D12 and C10 forbid. Expiry returns the row to `delivered`/`pending` **and re-delivers it under no registered cadence** (`forceDeliver`), which is what makes "`snoozed` is the only pause and it expires" true rather than nominal. A `seen` row snoozed through intake comes out with `expiresAt` **unset** — the able-to-fail case for D9's state invariant, which an enumeration of out-transitions omits and which would otherwise let a working row be TTL-deleted. Expiry returns the row to `delivered` when it has a recorded `accepted` outcome and to `pending` otherwise, and the row is nudge-eligible immediately by that fact rather than by a special case.
11. **AC11 (cadence sourcing)** — With no `ops_policy`, a row is **attempted exactly once per occurrence** and never nudged between occurrences — including after a `rejected` and after an `unknown` outcome, neither of which earns a re-attempt — and registering a cadence resumes nudging within one stall re-check interval, without a restart. **This is the deployment-gate test the merge-ahead-of-KPR-455 argument rests on, so it is driven against the real scan predicate, not a stub**, and it has three able-to-fail limbs, each false of a different plausible implementation:
    - *Never unset.* Between deliveries the row must still carry a **set** `nextNudgeAt`, and must be returned by `{ state: { $in: ["pending","delivered"] }, nextNudgeAt: { $lte: now } }` once the clock is advanced past its re-check — false of any implementation that unsets the field (MongoDB type bracketing; `fake-db.ts:40-41` reproduces it, so the double fails it too).
    - *Pushed forward, not left due.* Immediately after the first delivery the row must **not** be returned by that same predicate at the unadvanced clock — false of the "leave it at its due value" implementation, which is what puts an ever-growing, never-TTL'd prefix at the head of the delivery scan.
    - *The two re-delivery triggers still fire.* A row cleared and then renewed under no `ops_policy` is **delivered again** (`forceDeliver`, `attemptCount` now 2), and a row snoozed and then expired under no `ops_policy` is **delivered again** — both false of a two-disjunct `attemptCount === 0 ∨ cadence` gate, which under the shipped default turns a demonstrated recurrence and an expired snooze into permanent silence.

    A test then asserts the row is re-observed across successive re-check intervals — `rowsCadenceUnresolved` counts it each time, derived from the sparse `stalledReason` index rather than from the delivery pass — before asserting that the first re-check after a cadence is written delivers. A subscription naming a cadence profile uses that profile's interval and **never** the `(class, retry)` table; naming an **unknown** profile resolves no cadence and does not fall back to the table. A profile below the floor is clamped up and warned. This child registers zero `ops_policy` rows.
12. **AC12 (C13, redaction)** — No `ops_notifications` field contains message text, prompt or completion text, tool arguments, a raw error object, a URL, a credential, a file path or free-form prose. Negative test: a delivery whose transport fails with a credential-shaped and a path-shaped error stores neither substring anywhere on the row. `remediation` is a registry template plus allow-listed parameters, rendered **in the adapter** at delivery; the ledger stores no rendered string.
13. **AC13 (containment (b))** — **The two containment rules are asserted separately, because a single "per-row continue" implementation passes one and silently loses events on the other.** *Delivery and snooze expiry:* a per-row fault is caught and counted, the phase continues to the next row, and neither the tick nor the next tick is aborted. *Ingest:* a fault injected while applying the **third** event of a five-event page must leave the cursor at or before the **second** event's position (never past it), must leave events 3–5 unapplied, and the next tick must apply 3, 4 and 5 — the able-to-fail case is the per-row-continue implementation, which applies 4 and 5, advances the cursor to 5, and leaves event 3 permanently unnotified and invisible to `eventsBehind`. A second limb pins the event-level unit: a fault injected in the *second* row of a clearing fan-out for event `e` stops ingest at `e` and leaves the cursor before it, and the retry re-applies `e` with the already-cleared row a no-op via the watermark. Ticks never overlap under a slow adapter (a test starts a second tick while the first is in flight and asserts it is a no-op); ticks never overlap under a slow adapter (a test starts a second tick while the first is in flight and asserts it is a no-op); a per-row latch serializes an intake write against a tick on the same row, and the loser observes a CAS failure rather than a merged write.
14. **AC14 (boot order, drain, and boot survival)** — `src/boot-order.test.ts` carries `await opsNotifier.init()` and `setOpsNotifier(` in **all three** lists, and a new lifecycle block pins `registerTransport(` before `await opsNotifier.start()`, `await opsNotifier.start()` after both `dispatcher.registerAdapter(slackAdapter)` and `await slackAdapter.start(`, and `await opsNotifier.stop()` before `await slackAdapter.stop()` and `await mongoClient.close()`. **Negative-verify:** moving the wiring block below `await bgTaskManager.scanOrphans()` fails (b) *and* (c) while (a) stays green — predict both. A unique-index failure at `init()` leaves the notifier unstarted **with the singleton unset**, so `accept(...)` returns `{ state: "unavailable" }`, and boot completes; a failure of any other index leaves it started; a first-subscription-load failure in `start()` leaves it unstarted with intake live. **The `init()`-side reason-map fault is pinned separately and resolves the other way**: `setOpsNotifier` still runs, the sweep never starts, and `accept(...)` on an existing row **applies** — because reasons feed only `remediation` at delivery and intake consumes none of them. **Intake availability is pinned to `init()`, not `start()`:** with `init()` complete and `start()` never called, `accept(...)` on an existing row **applies** and no turn is affected; with the singleton unset, or after `stop()`, it returns `{ state: "unavailable" }`. A separate assertion pins the load's placement — no `ops_subscriptions` read occurs during `init()` — because a load that ran there would have no registered adapter to run `validateTarget` against and would unload every subscription on a warm restart.
15. **AC15 (C12)** — No code path in this diff reads `activity_log.error`, `costUsd`, or a turn/tool duration to decide that anything failed. Every ledger row originates in a published `ops_events` document, and `activity_log` is neither read nor written here.
16. **AC16 (C16, D11 levers)** — Adding a subscriber, a transport binding, a reason or a cadence value is data rather than an engine change: a test inserts an `ops_subscriptions` row and an `ops_policy` document into an already-initialized notifier and, after one reload, publishes an event through KPR-454's accept path and observes a row created, delivered and scheduled — with no edit to the envelope, the filter grammar or the transport interface. The reverse: setting `enabled: false` on that row stops delivery and nudging within one reload while retaining the row, and re-enabling resumes.
17. **AC17 (documentation)** — `CLAUDE.md` gains `ops_notifications` and `ops_policy` in the engine-written collections list with keys, indexes and TTL posture; the `telemetry` entry gains `ops_notifier_stats` **and `ops_sweep_cursor` marked as durable sweep progress rather than a heartbeat**, with the consequence of dropping the collection stated (cursor re-initializes to the clock; the gap's events are never notified, bounded and counted, and a restart does not recover them) — every other document in that collection is disposable stats and a reader must not generalize from them; a Common Gotchas bullet names the two levers, the no-cadence default in its precise "attempted once per occurrence" form, and the KPR-455 sequencing constraint.

## Assumptions and remaining decisions

- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — idempotence is apply-if-strictly-newer over `(publishedAt, _id)`, strengthening D12's `latestEventId` rule.** Argued in D3 and Key Points bullet 2 with the concrete C17 failure the weaker rule admits. This is a strengthening within the contract's own stated intent ("`eventCount` counts distinct events, never sweep attempts"), not an amendment; it costs two scalar fields on the row and makes any prefix replay a total no-op. AC4 is the able-to-fail test, and it predicts both wrong implementations (4 for the unconditional `$set`, 3 for the no-regress variant). **This spec cannot execute the recording, so it states the obligation instead: KPR-458's pending Task-2 addendum should carry one line noting that D12's `latestEventId`-equality phrasing is insufficient against C17 for a page in which one `dedupeKey` is touched twice, and that KPR-468 implements apply-if-strictly-newer over `(publishedAt, _id)`.** An addendum is the right instrument precisely because this is not an amendment — C17 is unchanged and the contract's intent is unchanged; what changes is the mechanism a child uses to satisfy it, which the next reader of `kpr-458-design.md` should not have to re-derive. The plan writer or the implementer picks this up when Task 2 runs.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — the D12 cursor lives in `db.telemetry` (`kind: "ops_sweep_cursor"`), not in a dedicated collection.** D12 explicitly leaves the location to this child and rules it is not a contract collection. Justified in D3: `telemetry` is engine-written, TTL-free and upserted per `kind`, and the cold-start rule makes cursor loss cost at most one sweep interval of events. The alternative — an `ops_notifier_state` collection — is one more collection, one more `CLAUDE.md` line, for a difference the initialization rule already erases. Reversible in one module.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — a missing cursor initializes to the clock, never to the log's beginning.** The strongest single argument for it is the staging story: KPR-454 merges first and accumulates, so a backfilling notifier would deliver weeks of history on its first tick and re-mint rows for conditions whose ledger rows had already aged out. The cost is a bounded, counted gap. If an operator ever genuinely wants a backfill, it is an explicit one-off cursor write, not a default.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — `ops_policy` as a new collection, and cadence read per tick rather than cached.** D5 and D11 make cadence values, profiles, the floor and the snooze maximum operator-registered data, and D1 named no home for them; one small document read once per 30 s tick is cheaper than a reload timer and is always fresh. **Zero rows ship.** The simpler alternative — code constants — would forfeit the operator lever entirely and would be exactly the default policy dependency KPR-456 canon forbids an engineering child from supplying.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — no cadence registered means attempted once per occurrence (delivered if the transport accepts), never nudged between occurrences.** The alternative is an engine default interval, which is policy this child may not hold. Stated as a deployment gate rather than a failure, and it is also what makes merging ahead of KPR-455 tolerable — a consequence, not a substitute for D11's sequencing constraint, whose residual is named there in full. **Its *realization* is not delegated and is fixed in D5**, because both natural implementations are silently wrong. Unsetting `nextNudgeAt` hides the row from the `$lte` due-scan under MongoDB's type bracketing (`fake-db.ts:40-41` reproduces it), stranding it permanently. Leaving it at its due value parks a never-TTL'd, monotonically growing set permanently at the head of that scan (working-state rows carry no `expiresAt`, D9), which starves the one guaranteed delivery the merge-ahead argument rests on — silently, since the row simply stays `pending`. The row is therefore **stalled forward** on a bounded re-check with a `stalledReason` marker, the scan is split into three arms whose first two are index-immune to the stall set, and the attempt gate is `attemptCount === 0 ∨ forceDeliver ∨ a cadence resolved`. AC11 and AC7 drive all of it against the real predicate.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — the stall re-check interval (~5 minutes) and its jitter fraction.** A **re-evaluation backoff, not a delivery cadence**: it decides neither whether, nor to whom, nor how often anything is delivered — only how soon a declined decision is re-evaluated — so it is not the engine default policy KPR-456 canon forbids an engineering child from supplying. (It is *not* true that nothing is delivered on it: a first delivery stalled on an unresolved subscription or an unbound adapter happens on a re-check tick. See D5.) It sets D11 levers 1 and 3's *resume* latency (the *stop* direction is unaffected), and jitter exists so a cohort stalled on one tick does not re-arrive as one herd. Shorter costs scan; longer costs lever latency; neither is a correctness boundary, which is why the value is delegated and the mechanism is not.
- **Non-blocking, follows from the contract — a sparse `forceDeliver` covers the two non-cadence re-delivery triggers.** Not a delegated call: without it, the shipped default turns D4's `cleared → pending` reopen and D7's snooze expiry into permanent silence, because `attemptCount` is monotonic and neither arm resets it. That contradicts D4's own "errs toward a duplicate rather than a gap", KPR-458 D7's "a reopened `cleared` row is `pending`, so its next delivery goes through the ordinary attempt path", and C10's "`snoozed` is the only pause **and it expires**". Written at exactly two arms, cleared by the attempt that consumes it (and by an intake `seen`/`dismissed`), never cleared by a skip. AC11's third limb and AC10 are the able-to-fail tests.
- **Non-blocking, follows from the contract — every clock-bearing intake write is anchored at `min(at, now)`.** `at` is caller-supplied and D6 opens intake to an agent tool call, so an unanchored ceiling is bypassable and the clamp that "must always exist" (D12) becomes advisory. Anchoring satisfies KPR-458's "measured from the instant of the act" for any honest `at`, costs one `min`, and closes the same hole for `stateAt`/`expiresAt` (D9's per-person retention horizon) and for `lastAckAt`. `lastAckKey` keeps the raw `at`, since an idempotence key must be a pure function of the caller's input.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — no retry ladder ships; `rejected` is recorded and the next attempt is the next nudge.** The contract decides most of this: D6's `{ status: "rejected"; reason }` carries no retry hint, so there is nothing to schedule from, and C8 *permits* rather than requires an automatic retry. Inventing a backoff would be cadence policy by another name. Cost: a `refused-rate-limit` row waits a full cadence interval — and **under no registered cadence that wait is unbounded**, because the attempt consumed the occurrence's only trigger and the next attempt is the row's next occurrence (a recurrence after a clear, or a snooze expiry), which may never come. Reversible if a hint is ever added to the outcome type — which would be a contract amendment, not a change here.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — the `judgment` / `integrity` clearing provenance test requires at least one `evidence` reference.** D3's wording for `integrity` ("carrying the underlying authoritative evidence") reads almost literally onto it; for `judgment` ("an event recording a named human ruling") it is an inference that the ruling's record is the reference. The envelope offers no other checkable signal, and this component may not judge content. The stated alternative is to accept C19's registration link alone as sufficient for all three classes, which is one predicate looser and is a one-line change. Named because a reviewer should look at it.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — ledger TTL is `min(30 days, config.activity.retentionDays)`.** D9 flags the retention posture as a privacy call wanting operator confirmation and rules that the behavioural half must age before the event log; the `min` keeps that true for any operator setting with no new key. The value is the **acknowledgement-silence horizon**, not only a privacy setting (D9), and raising it is a code change.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — a contract-side hard snooze ceiling (7 days) exists in code so a clamp is always available.** D12 makes the clamp mandatory and its maximum operator data this child ships none of; without a code-side ceiling an unregistered deployment would admit an unbounded snooze, i.e. the terminal state ruling 2 forbids. A registered maximum can only tighten it. This is a bound, not a cadence.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — one transport ships, and it is Slack.** The alternative — interface only — was considered and declined on three grounds: D6's three-outcome taxonomy would have no vendor mapping to test, so C8 would be untestable; this ticket's own boot-order obligation ("the sweep starts after adapter registration") would be vacuous; and shipping it costs nothing operationally, since with zero subscription rows nothing is ever delivered regardless of which adapters are registered. The scope guard is that **exactly one** adapter ships and `SlackObligationPoster` is neither reused nor refactored.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — intake idempotence keys on `(row, act, actorId, at)`, with `at` the vendor act's instant.** D12 says "`(row, act, actor)`", which cannot distinguish two genuinely distinct snoozes by the same actor — and D7 explicitly permits re-snoozing to move `snoozedUntil`. Including the act's own instant resolves the two, and it is why D6 fixes `at` as part of the intake tuple. Residual: an inbound edge that substitutes its own receipt time weakens idempotence to a harmless double-apply, and only for snooze — a repeated `seen` or `dismissed` lands on a row already in that state and changes nothing but `stateAt`. Non-blocking, and it is an obligation on KPR-455's edge worth stating in that child's spec. **The key is now paired with a `lastAckAt` monotonicity guard** (D7 step 3), because the key alone has exactly the weakness D3 rejects for events — it records only the *last* act, so a delayed duplicate of an **older** one passes it, passes legality, and regresses the row. Named residual of the guard itself: two distinct acts bearing an identical `at` resolve to a no-op on the second, which errs toward not-applied for an act the actor can repeat.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — the acknowledgement handle is the row `_id`'s hex string and confers no authority.** Attribution, not authorization, is the contract's model (D9). Named residual: a handle round-tripped through a vendor payload can be altered by the actor, so an act can in principle be applied to a row the actor was not shown — attributed to them either way. Adding an authorization model would be policy this component may not hold; a signed handle is the reversible alternative if the residual ever matters.
- **⚠ Delegated (non-blocking) — the numeric bounds carry no value in this spec** and are the plan writer's: sweep page sizes and the per-tick event budget, the delivery phase's wall-clock budget and inter-attempt spacing, the intake deadline, the saturating `limit` on `eventsBehind`'s and the two gauges' `countDocuments`, the stall re-check interval and its jitter (its own bullet above), and the `attempts[]` ring cap (stated as 5 above, and the one this spec does fix). What is **not** delegated is the behaviour on breach, fixed in D3 and D5: leftover work defers to the next tick and is visible as backlog; nothing is dropped; nothing is skipped. Nor is `eventsBehind`'s *form* delegated — D5 pins it to a bounded, saturating count plus an always-reported `oldestUnappliedAt` (free when the tick stopped inside a page, one extra bounded indexed `findOne` on a page-boundary or contained-fault stop, never omitted), because the obvious unbounded `countDocuments` over everything past the cursor adds the most load exactly when the sweep is furthest behind, and because an age compared against the event TTL is the better signal for the one gap it exists to expose. No `hive.yaml` key ships for any of them.
- **Non-blocking here, but a stated precondition on the next child — the cursor's no-skip guarantee holds only while KPR-454 is the sole producer.** Named in D3 in full. `publishedAt` is stamped at accept and the insert lands later, so a second concurrent producer could insert an event already behind an advanced cursor; it would never be notified, and — unlike the TTL-outrun gap, which `oldestUnappliedAt` at least measures — it is invisible to every signal here. KPR-458 forecasts that producer (D10's "KPR-456's sweep is, later, a natural *producer* into this log"; D2's "a producer that does not exist yet must be addable as data"), so this is a residual of the same class as the TTL gap and is named on the same terms rather than left as an inherited surprise. Nothing is done about it in this child because no second producer exists; the child that adds one must choose serialization through the same drainer, a cursor safety lag, or a measured-and-accepted skip.
- **Non-blocking, follows from the contract — clearing is applied outside `matchedSubscriptionIds`.** Not a delegated call: D12's own guarantee that recovering `resource` conditions fall silent on their own is false under any match-scoped reading, because clearing reasons are `informational` and normally match nobody. AC6 is the able-to-fail test.
- **Non-blocking, follows from the contract — `NotificationView` carries the resolved target.** `deliver` takes exactly one argument, so the target must ride the view; D6 bars an adapter from *choosing* a recipient, not from being handed one.
- **Non-blocking — the unique index carries a correctness role, so its failure leaves the notifier unstarted.** This is the one place this child's index posture differs from KPR-454's blanket "contained and stay wired": the `(subscriptionId, dedupeKey)` uniqueness is the ledger's identity guarantee, and running without it means duplicate rows and duplicate nudges for one condition. Boot is still never fatal.
- **Non-blocking — the KPR-458 addendum is not yet on disk.** The design body at this commit still reads "chartered to no child" for D12 and D6; `kpr-458-plan.md:13` carries the operator's resolution and its Task 2 appends the addendum. This spec is written against the resolution. A reader who opens `kpr-458-design.md` alone will find the stale text until that plan runs.
- **Non-blocking — this child depends on KPR-454 as planned, not as merged.** Every module, type and index it consumes is specified in `kpr-454-design.md` and its six plan chunks at this commit and does not exist in the tree yet. If KPR-454's implementation diverges on the `OpsStore` surface, the `ops_events` cursor index, or the `matchedSubscriptionIds` stamp, this spec's integration points are where the divergence lands and the plan writer should re-verify all three against the merged code before writing a task.
- **Plan shape — this is a 4–6 chunk plan, not one.** At its current length the spec has not outgrown a single ticket, but it has outgrown a single-chunk implementation plan. The natural seams, in dependency order: (1) `src/ops/notification-types.ts`, the `OpsTransport`/`NotificationView`/`DeliveryOutcome` transcription and the Slack adapter with its vendor mapping and echo registration; (2) the ledger store — collection, the nine indexes, the `db.telemetry` cursor, the ingest phase with its event-level containment and the apply-if-newer watermark; (3) delivery, nudging, the three scan arms, the stall, snooze expiry and the heartbeat; (4) intake — the check order, the clock anchor, the clamp, the CAS and the per-row latch; (5) boot wiring, `boot-order.test.ts`'s three lists plus the lifecycle block, the shutdown drain and `CLAUDE.md`. A sixth chunk for the acceptance suite is reasonable if (2)–(4) each carry only their own unit tests. Chunks 2, 3 and 4 all touch the same row shape, so the type module in chunk 1 is what keeps them independent; do not attempt one chunk.
- **Blocking for deployment, not for this spec — KPR-468 must not ship without KPR-455's inbound acknowledgement edge.** D12's constraint and this ticket's own body. D11 states the two properties that make the *merge* honest and names the residual that remains: an operator who registers both a subscription and a cadence before the edge lands gets unbounded nudging on `judgment`, `integrity` and never-recovering `resource` rows, with the per-subscription lever as the only stop.
